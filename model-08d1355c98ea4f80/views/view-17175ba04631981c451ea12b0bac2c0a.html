Workforce Relations Mgt ()
## Purpose This process ensures that all workforce relations activities—collective bargaining, grievance handling, and compliance monitoring—are conducted in a fair, transparent, and legally compliant manner. It engages trade unions and staff representatives through structured steps, from initial notification to final resolution and record-keeping, fostering a cooperative work environment. ## Roles - HR Relations Manager: Oversees the entire process, approves settlements and final reports. - HR Relations Officer: Handles day-to-day case management, investigation, and documentation. - Legal Advisor: Provides legal guidance on compliance and collective agreement interpretation. - Trade Union Representative: Participates in bargaining and grievance hearings on behalf of employees. - System: Automated notifications, document storage, and compliance checks. ## Upstream Inputs - Communication Records: Emails, meeting minutes, and correspondence with unions and staff. - Policy Documentation: Current HR policies, collective agreements, and labour law references. - Grievance Notification: Formal complaint submitted by an employee or union. - Bargaining Mandate: Official request from union or management to commence negotiations. ## Downstream Outputs - Grievance Resolution Report: Final outcome of grievance investigation and hearing. - Collective Bargaining Agreement: Signed and approved contract terms. - Compliance Monitoring Report: Summary of compliance status and any corrective actions. - Archived Case File: Complete record of all documents and decisions for audit. ## Key Decisions - Determine if the issue is a grievance or a bargaining trigger. - Assess grievance validity and whether it requires formal hearing. - Evaluate bargaining proposals and counter-offers during negotiation. - Approve or reject the final settlement or resolution. - Decide if compliance monitoring reveals non-compliance requiring corrective action. ## Exception Paths - Grievance deemed invalid: Returned to initiator with explanation; process ends. - Grievance investigation finds no violation: Case closed with no further action. - Bargaining negotiation deadlock: Escalated to mediation or arbitration. - Compliance non-compliance detected: Initiate corrective action loop back to remediation step. - Final approval rejected: Return to negotiation or investigation for rework. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Notification: The HR Relations Officer receives a formal notification—either a grievance complaint from an employee/union or a bargaining mandate from management or union. The notification is entered into the case management system with a unique ID, date, and source. Communication records such as emails or meeting minutes are attached as evidence. 2. Review Documentation: The HR Relations Officer retrieves relevant policy documentation, collective agreements, and labour law references. They cross-reference the notification details with existing policies to understand obligations, timelines, and precedents. Any missing documents are requested from the relevant parties. 3. Assess Issue Type: The HR Relations Officer evaluates the nature of the notification based on the content and source. If it involves a complaint about working conditions, treatment, or policy violation, it is classified as a grievance. If it is a request to negotiate new terms or renew an agreement, it is classified as a bargaining trigger. This decision determines the next process branch. 4. Investigate Grievance: For grievance cases, the HR Relations Officer conducts a formal investigation. They interview the complainant, the respondent, and any witnesses. Evidence such as emails, performance records, and CCTV footage is collected. All findings are documented in an investigation report. 5. Conduct Hearing: A formal hearing is scheduled with the employee, union representative, HR Relations Manager, and Legal Advisor. The investigation findings are presented, and both sides are given an opportunity to respond. The hearing panel deliberates and produces a resolution recommendation. 6. Negotiate Terms: For bargaining triggers, the HR Relations Officer and Trade Union Representative engage in negotiation sessions. Proposals and counter-proposals are exchanged, and terms are drafted iteratively. Minutes of each session are recorded. The process continues until a provisional agreement is reached or a deadlock is declared. 7. Evaluate Proposal: The Legal Advisor examines the drafted agreement terms or hearing outcome for legal compliance, alignment with labour laws, and fairness to all parties. They may suggest revisions or flag risks. A compliance opinion is issued. 8. Approve Resolution: The HR Relations Manager reviews the compliance opinion and the final resolution report or agreement. If satisfied, they formally approve it. If not, they return it with comments for rework. Approval triggers the implementation and monitoring phase. 9. Monitor Compliance: The system automatically monitors compliance with the approved grievance resolution or collective bargaining agreement. It checks for timely implementation, adherence to terms, and any new related incidents. A compliance report is generated periodically. 10. Address Non-Compliance: If the compliance report flags any deviation, the HR Relations Officer initiates a corrective action plan. This may involve re-education, process changes, or disciplinary measures. The case is re-monitored after corrective actions are implemented. 11. Archive Records: All documents related to the case—including the initial notification, investigation findings, hearing outcome, agreement, compliance reports, and corrective actions—are compiled and archived in the HR document management system. The case is marked as closed.
