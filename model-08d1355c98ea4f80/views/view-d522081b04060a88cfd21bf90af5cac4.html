## Purpose Benefits Management ensures that the university systematically identifies, defines, tracks, and reports on the realisation of expected benefits from strategic initiatives and projects. It provides accountability and visibility into value delivery, enabling timely corrective action when benefits deviate from planned targets. ## Roles - Benefits Manager: owns the process, facilitates benefit identification and tracking - Project Manager: provides project plans and status updates - Finance Analyst: supplies performance and financial data - Executive Sponsor: reviews reports and approves corrective action plans - System: automated tracking and reporting platform ## Upstream Inputs - Business Case - Project Plan - Performance Data - Benefit Definition Template ## Downstream Outputs - Benefits Realisation Report - Corrective Action Plan - Benefit Tracking Dashboard ## Key Decisions - Are benefits clearly defined and measurable? - Are benefits on track to meet targets? - What corrective actions are required to address benefit gaps? - Should the initiative be escalated or closed due to benefit shortfall? ## Exception Paths - If benefits are not measurable, they are redefined with clearer metrics. - If benefits are significantly off-track, a corrective action plan is created and tracked. - If benefits cannot be recovered, the initiative is escalated to the Executive Sponsor for closure. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Identify Expected Benefits: The Benefits Manager examines the approved business case and project plan to extract all stated or implied benefits. Each benefit is documented with a description, owner, and alignment to institutional strategic goals. This step ensures no benefit is overlooked before tracking begins. 2. Define Benefit Metrics: The Benefits Manager collaborates with the Project Manager to translate each benefit into one or more measurable key performance indicators (KPIs). Targets, measurement methods, and data sources are agreed upon. This step ensures benefits are objectively trackable. 3. Baseline Benefit Targets: The Benefits Manager enters the baseline current value, target value, and target realisation date for each benefit metric into the Benefits Tracking System. This establishes a reference point for future performance comparisons. 4. Collect Performance Data: The Finance Analyst extracts actual performance data from relevant operational systems (e.g., finance, student information, HR) and uploads it to the Benefits Tracking System. Data is validated for completeness and accuracy before use. 5. Assess Benefit Realisation: The Benefits Manager reviews the actual performance data against the established baselines and targets. Each benefit is assessed as on track, at risk, or off track. This evaluation determines the next steps in the process. 6. Generate Benefits Report: When benefits are assessed as on track, the system automatically generates a Benefits Realisation Report summarising progress against targets, including visual dashboards and commentary. The report is distributed to stakeholders. 7. Initiate Corrective Action: When benefits are assessed as off track, the Benefits Manager works with the Project Manager to identify root causes and define corrective actions. A Corrective Action Plan is documented with owners, timelines, and revised targets. 8. Track Corrective Actions: The Benefits Manager tracks the progress of each corrective action, updating the tracking dashboard with status, completion dates, and any changes to benefit forecasts. Regular check-ins with the Project Manager ensure accountability. 9. Escalate or Close Initiative: If corrective actions fail to bring benefits back on track, the Executive Sponsor reviews the situation and decides whether to escalate the issue to a higher governance body or close the initiative. A closure report is produced if applicable.
