Curriculum Change Mgt ()
## Purpose This process governs the submission, review, approval, and implementation of changes to existing curricula. It ensures that all modifications are academically justified, compliant with external regulations, and informed by student outcomes, while maintaining clear documentation and stakeholder communication. ## Roles - Academic Staff: Submits the initial change proposal and may be required to rework it. - Curriculum Reviewer: Assesses the proposal for completeness and regulatory alignment. - Curriculum Committee: Provides formal approval for the change. - Curriculum Administrator: Produces and distributes updated documentation. - System: Automates notifications and document generation. ## Upstream Inputs - Curriculum Change Proposal (from Academic Staff) - Regulatory Requirements (external) - Student Outcome Data (from institutional systems) ## Downstream Outputs - Updated Curriculum Documentation - Stakeholder Notification - Change Implementation Record ## Key Decisions - Is the proposal complete and valid? - Does the proposal meet regulatory requirements? - Are student outcomes positively impacted? - Should the proposal be approved, rejected, or reworked? ## Exception Paths - If the proposal is incomplete, it is returned to the submitter for rework. - If the proposal fails regulatory or outcome assessment, it is rejected and the submitter is notified. - If the Curriculum Committee rejects the proposal, the process ends with a rejection notification. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Submit Change Proposal: Academic staff member submits a formal curriculum change proposal via the system. 2. Validate Proposal: Curriculum Reviewer checks the proposal for completeness and validity; if incomplete, it is sent back for rework. 3. Assess Regulatory Compliance: Curriculum Reviewer evaluates the proposal against external regulatory requirements; if non-compliant, it is rejected. 4. Review Student Outcomes: Curriculum Reviewer analyzes student outcome data to assess the impact of the proposed change; if negative impact is found, the proposal is rejected. 5. Approve Change: Curriculum Committee reviews the consolidated assessment and makes a final approval or rejection decision. 6. Produce Updated Documentation: Curriculum Administrator generates the revised curriculum documents based on the approved change. 7. Communicate Change: System sends notifications and the updated documentation to all relevant stakeholders. Steps: 1. Submit Change Proposal: An academic staff member identifies a need for a curriculum change and completes a standardized proposal form in the curriculum management system. The proposal includes the rationale, affected courses, and expected outcomes. The submission triggers a notification to the Curriculum Reviewer. 2. Validate Proposal: The Curriculum Reviewer examines the submitted proposal to ensure all required fields are filled, supporting documents are attached, and the change is within scope. If the proposal is incomplete or invalid, it is returned to the submitter with comments. If valid, it proceeds to assessment. 3. Assess Regulatory Compliance: The Curriculum Reviewer maps the proposed change against current regulatory standards and accreditation requirements. A compliance report is generated. If the proposal fails to meet any mandatory requirement, it is flagged for rejection. If compliant, it moves to outcome review. 4. Review Student Outcomes: The Curriculum Reviewer examines historical student outcome data (e.g., pass rates, satisfaction scores) related to the curriculum area being changed. A data-driven impact analysis is produced. If the change is likely to negatively affect outcomes, the proposal is flagged for rejection. Otherwise, it proceeds to the approval stage. 5. Approve Change: The Curriculum Committee reviews the compliance assessment and outcome impact analysis. They deliberate and vote on the proposal. The decision is recorded in the system. If approved, the change moves to documentation. If rejected, the submitter is notified with reasons. 6. Produce Updated Documentation: The Curriculum Administrator uses the approved change details to update all relevant curriculum documentation, including course descriptions, learning outcomes, and assessment plans. The system generates a version-controlled document set. 7. Communicate Change: The system automatically sends notifications and the updated documentation to all relevant stakeholders, including academic staff, students, and accreditation bodies. A change implementation record is archived for audit purposes. 8. Rework Proposal: The academic staff member receives feedback from the validation step and revises the proposal accordingly. The updated proposal is resubmitted for re-validation. This step ensures all proposals meet minimum quality standards before full assessment.
