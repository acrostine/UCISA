## Purpose This process manages the end-to-end lifecycle of institutional content, from ingestion through classification, review, storage, and delivery. It ensures that digital and physical content is organised, accessible, and compliant with regulatory and operational standards. ## Roles - Content Steward: Submits and classifies content - Content Reviewer: Reviews content for quality and compliance - Content Approver: Approves content for publication - Repository Manager: Manages storage and retention - System: Automates metadata assignment, retention enforcement, and delivery ## Upstream Inputs - Content Submission (documents, multimedia, publications) - Metadata Templates - Retention Policy Rules - Access Control Lists ## Downstream Outputs - Classified Content Package - Approved Content Record - Published Content Repository - Delivered Content Package - Audit Log Entry ## Key Decisions - Is the content complete and correctly classified? - Does the content meet quality and compliance standards? - Is the content approved for publication? - What retention period applies? ## Exception Paths - Content fails classification: returned to submitter for rework - Content fails review: sent back for correction or rejected - Content approval denied: archived as rejected with reason - Retention expiry triggers automatic archival or deletion ## Step Walkthrough 1. Submit Content: Content Steward uploads or submits digital or physical content into the system. 2. Classify Content: Content Steward assigns metadata and content type based on templates. 3. Assign Metadata: System automatically enriches the record with system-generated metadata and retention rules. 4. Review Content: Content Reviewer checks for quality, completeness, and compliance. 5. Approve Content: Content Approver decides whether to approve the content for publication. 6. Store Content: Repository Manager stores the approved content in the structured repository with access controls. 7. Publish Content: System makes the content available through delivery mechanisms to authorised users. 8. Deliver Content: System delivers the content package to requesting users or systems. 9. Log Audit Entry: System records all actions in the audit log for compliance. Steps: 1. Submit Content: The Content Steward uploads documents, multimedia files, or publications via the content management interface. Physical content is logged with a digital placeholder. The submission triggers the classification workflow. 2. Classify Content: Using predefined metadata templates, the Content Steward selects the appropriate content type (e.g., document, multimedia, publication) and fills in required fields such as title, author, department, and keywords. 3. Assign Metadata: The system applies additional metadata such as creation date, file size, checksum, and retention period based on content type and institutional policy. Access control lists are also assigned. 4. Review Content: The Content Reviewer examines the content and its metadata to ensure it meets institutional quality standards, is complete, and complies with regulatory requirements. Any issues are flagged for correction. 5. Approve Content: The Content Approver reviews the reviewed content and either approves it for publication or rejects it with a reason. Approval grants permission for the content to be stored and made accessible. 6. Store Content: The Repository Manager places the approved content into the appropriate repository location, applying the assigned access controls and retention rules. The content is indexed for search and retrieval. 7. Publish Content: The system activates the content for access, updating search indexes and delivery channels. Authorised users can now retrieve the content based on their permissions. 8. Deliver Content: Upon request, the system delivers the content package to the authorised user or downstream system via secure download, streaming, or API. Delivery is logged for audit purposes. 9. Log Audit Entry: The system writes a final audit entry capturing the entire content lifecycle from submission to delivery, including timestamps, roles, and decisions. This supports regulatory compliance and reporting. 10. Rework Content: The Content Steward revises the content or metadata based on feedback from the reviewer or classification errors, then resubmits for another review cycle. 11. Archive Rejected Content: The system moves rejected content to an archive with a rejection reason and timestamp, preserving it for audit purposes but removing it from active workflows.
