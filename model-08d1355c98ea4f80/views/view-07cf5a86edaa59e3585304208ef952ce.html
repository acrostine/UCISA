Curriculum & Resource Development ()
## Purpose This process governs the systematic creation and revision of curriculum content and its supporting learning resources. It ensures that educational offerings remain current, relevant, and aligned with institutional goals by leveraging research, industry insights, and direct student input. The process covers content drafting, resource identification, approval gates, and final quality checks. ## Roles - Curriculum Lead: Oversees content development and alignment with standards. - Subject Matter Expert (SME): Provides academic and industry expertise. - Instructional Designer: Designs and develops learning resources and digital tools. - Quality Reviewer: Conducts final quality assurance checks. - Approver (Dean/Head): Authorises final curriculum and resource package. ## Upstream Inputs - Academic Research Summaries - Industry Trend Reports - Student Feedback Data - Existing Curriculum Documents - Resource Inventory List ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Curriculum Content - Developed Learning Resources (teaching materials, digital tools) - Curriculum-Resource Alignment Matrix - Deployment-Ready Package ## Key Decisions - Whether curriculum content requires major revision or minor update. - Which learning resources need to be created vs. sourced externally. - Approval of final curriculum and resource package for deployment. ## Exception Paths - If content fails SME review, it is returned for revision with specific feedback. - If resources are not aligned with curriculum goals, they are reworked or replaced. - If quality assurance identifies gaps, the package is sent back for remediation before final approval. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Gather Inputs: The Curriculum Lead compiles recent academic research papers, industry trend reports, and aggregated student feedback from surveys and course evaluations. These inputs are organised and summarised to inform the curriculum development process. 2. Draft Curriculum Content: Using the compiled input summary, the Curriculum Lead drafts or updates curriculum content including learning objectives, module outlines, and assessment criteria. The draft is structured to align with institutional standards and educational goals. 3. Review Content by SME: The SME reviews the curriculum content draft for academic rigour, alignment with current research, and industry relevance. They provide feedback and either approve or request revisions. 4. Revise Content: If the SME identifies issues, the Curriculum Lead revises the content to address all feedback points. The revised draft is then resubmitted for SME review. 5. Identify Required Resources: The Instructional Designer, in consultation with the Curriculum Lead, identifies teaching materials, digital tools, and other resources required to deliver the curriculum effectively. This includes textbooks, software, videos, and assessment tools. 6. Develop or Source Resources: The Instructional Designer develops new resources (e.g., slide decks, handouts, digital modules) or sources existing ones from libraries, vendors, or open educational repositories. All resources are aligned with curriculum objectives. 7. Approve Curriculum & Resources: The Approver reviews the final curriculum content and developed resources together, ensuring coherence and alignment with institutional strategy. They either approve the package for deployment or return it with comments. 8. Perform Quality Assurance: The Quality Reviewer examines the approved package for completeness, consistency, and readiness. They verify that all resources are correctly linked to curriculum modules and that no errors remain. 9. Remediate QA Findings: If the QA report identifies gaps or errors, the Curriculum Lead and Instructional Designer collaboratively remediate the issues. Corrections are applied to content or resources as needed. 10. Finalise Package: After successful QA or remediation, the package is formally finalised in the system. It is tagged as 'Ready for Deployment' and made available for implementation in the teaching schedule.
