## Purpose The Curriculum Design process establishes a structured approach to developing new curriculum structures and course frameworks. It ensures that all programs are built on a foundation of academic rigor, industry relevance, and pedagogical innovation, ultimately producing detailed course specifications and measurable learning outcomes that align with institutional goals and enhance graduate employability. ## Roles - Curriculum Designer: Leads the drafting and development of curriculum frameworks and course specifications. - Subject Matter Expert (SME): Provides academic and disciplinary expertise during review and validation. - Industry Advisor: Offers employer perspectives and ensures relevance to workforce needs. - Curriculum Committee: Reviews and approves curriculum proposals. - System: Supports automated notifications and document management. ## Upstream Inputs - Academic Standards Documentation - Employer Needs Assessment Report - Institutional Strategic Plan - Pedagogical Best Practice Guidelines - Existing Program Review Findings ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Curriculum Framework - Detailed Course Specifications - Defined Learning Outcomes - Assessment Strategy Document - Program Approval Package ## Key Decisions - Whether the draft curriculum framework meets academic and industry standards - Whether detailed course specifications are complete and aligned with learning outcomes - Whether the final curriculum package is ready for implementation ## Exception Paths - If the draft framework fails SME review, it is returned for revision with feedback. - If industry feedback identifies gaps, the framework is revised before resubmission. - If the curriculum committee rejects the proposal, a formal revision cycle is initiated. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Gather Inputs: The Curriculum Designer collects academic standards documentation, employer needs assessment reports, the institutional strategic plan, and pedagogical best practice guidelines. These inputs form the evidence base for all subsequent design decisions. All documents are cataloged and made available to the design team. 2. Draft Curriculum Framework: Using the gathered inputs, the Curriculum Designer develops a draft curriculum framework that includes program goals, course sequencing, credit allocations, and high-level learning outcomes. The draft is structured to align with institutional strategy and pedagogical best practices. This framework serves as the blueprint for detailed course development. 3. Review Framework with SMEs: The draft curriculum framework is sent to subject matter experts who assess its academic coherence, alignment with disciplinary standards, and pedagogical soundness. SMEs provide detailed feedback and a validation decision. If the framework is not validated, it is returned for revision. 4. Validate with Industry Advisors: The draft framework is shared with industry advisors who evaluate its alignment with current and emerging employer needs, industry standards, and graduate employability requirements. Advisors provide feedback and a relevance assessment. If gaps are identified, the framework is flagged for revision. 5. Revise Framework: The Curriculum Designer reviews all feedback from SMEs and industry advisors and makes necessary revisions to the curriculum framework. This may involve adjusting course sequences, updating learning outcomes, or adding new content areas. The revised framework is then prepared for formal approval. 6. Approve Curriculum Framework: The revised curriculum framework is submitted to the curriculum committee for formal review and approval. The committee evaluates the framework against institutional standards, strategic goals, and quality criteria. If approved, the framework becomes the official basis for detailed course development. If rejected, it is returned with reasons for further revision. 7. Develop Course Specifications: Using the approved curriculum framework, the Curriculum Designer develops detailed course specifications for each course. These include learning outcomes, content outlines, teaching methods, assessment strategies, and resource requirements. Each specification is designed to be coherent with the overall program goals and learning outcomes. 8. Review Course Specifications: The draft course specifications are reviewed by subject matter experts and the curriculum design team to ensure they accurately reflect the approved framework and meet quality standards. Reviewers check for completeness, alignment with learning outcomes, and feasibility. If issues are found, specifications are returned for revision. 9. Finalize Curriculum Package: Once course specifications are approved, the Curriculum Designer compiles the final curriculum package, including the approved framework, all course specifications, learning outcomes, and assessment strategies. The package is formatted for distribution to academic departments, accreditation bodies, and implementation teams. This marks the end of the design process.
