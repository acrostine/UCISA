Workforce Reporting ()
## Purpose Workforce Reporting ensures that HR data from personnel records, employee surveys, and operational metrics is systematically aggregated, validated, and transformed into actionable reports and dashboards. These outputs support leadership in monitoring compliance, diversity, and performance, thereby fostering transparency and evidence-based decision-making. ## Roles - HR Analyst: Collects, validates, and integrates data; generates reports and dashboards - HR Manager: Reviews and approves reports for accuracy and completeness - System: Automates data extraction, cleansing, and dashboard generation - Leadership: Consumes final reports and dashboards for strategic decisions ## Upstream Inputs - Personnel Records: Employee demographics, job history, compensation data - Survey Results: Employee engagement, satisfaction, and feedback data - Operational Metrics: Headcount, turnover, absenteeism, performance ratings - Reporting Request: Specific report or dashboard requirements from leadership ## Downstream Outputs - Compliance Reports: Regulatory filings, audit-ready data packs - Diversity Statistics: Workforce composition by gender, ethnicity, age, etc. - Performance Dashboards: Interactive visualizations of KPIs and trends - Published Reports: Finalized documents distributed to stakeholders ## Key Decisions - Data Completeness Check: Is all required data available and valid? - Report Accuracy Review: Do the generated reports meet quality and accuracy standards? - Approval Gate: Should the report be published as-is or require revision? ## Exception Paths - Missing or Incomplete Data: If required data is unavailable, the analyst initiates a data collection request and the process pauses until data is received. - Data Quality Issues: If validation fails, the analyst corrects source data or excludes invalid records, then re-runs the integration. - Report Rejection: If the HR Manager rejects the report, the analyst revises the report based on feedback and resubmits for approval. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Reporting Request: The HR analyst receives a reporting request via email or ticketing system. The request specifies the report type (compliance, diversity, or performance), required data fields, time period, and delivery deadline. The analyst confirms receipt and clarifies any ambiguities with the requester. 2. Extract Source Data: The system connects to the HRIS, survey platform, and operational data warehouse to extract the required data. Extraction is performed using pre-configured queries that pull data for the specified time period and scope. The system logs extraction results and any errors encountered. 3. Validate Data Quality: The analyst reviews the extracted data for missing fields, outliers, or inconsistencies. They compare record counts against expected totals and verify key metrics. Any data quality issues are documented for remediation. 4. Integrate and Cleanse Data: The system performs data integration by joining records on employee IDs and time periods. It removes duplicate entries, standardizes date formats and categorical values, and applies business rules to calculate derived fields. The cleansed dataset is stored in a reporting-ready format. 5. Generate Reports and Dashboards: The system runs pre-built report templates against the cleansed dataset. Compliance reports are generated in PDF format, diversity statistics in spreadsheet format, and performance dashboards as interactive web visualizations. All outputs are timestamped and versioned. 6. Review Report Accuracy: The analyst examines each output for data accuracy, formatting consistency, and alignment with the original request. They spot-check key figures against source data and verify that all required sections are present. Any issues are noted for correction. 7. Approve Report for Publication: The HR manager examines the reviewed reports and dashboards for strategic alignment, completeness, and professional quality. They either approve the outputs for distribution or reject them with specific revision feedback. 8. Distribute and Publish Reports: The analyst sends compliance reports and diversity statistics to designated recipients via email or secure file share. Performance dashboards are published to the internal reporting portal with appropriate access controls. A notification is sent to leadership confirming availability. 9. Address Data Quality Issues: When data quality issues are identified, the analyst contacts data owners to correct source records or applies exclusion rules to remove invalid entries. The corrected data is re-submitted for integration and cleansing. 10. Revise Reports Based on Feedback: The analyst reviews the rejection feedback and makes necessary corrections to the reports or dashboards. This may involve adjusting data filters, reformatting outputs, or recalculating metrics. Revised versions are re-submitted for approval.
