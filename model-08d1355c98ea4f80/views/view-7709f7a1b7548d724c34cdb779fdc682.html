## Purpose This process systematically identifies, assesses, and mitigates risks that could hinder the institution from achieving its strategic objectives. It consumes data from internal audits, external environment scans, and strategic plans to build a comprehensive risk picture. The output is a set of approved mitigation actions and an updated risk register that supports informed decision-making and protects institutional assets. ## Roles - Risk Officer: Facilitates risk identification, assessment, and mitigation planning - Risk Committee: Reviews high-priority risks and approves mitigation strategies - System: Automates risk scoring, notification, and register updates - Internal Audit: Provides audit findings as input to risk identification - Executive Sponsor: Escalates risks that exceed institutional risk appetite ## Upstream Inputs - Internal Audit Findings - External Environment Scan Reports - Strategic Plan Objectives - Risk Appetite Statement - Previous Risk Register ## Downstream Outputs - Updated Risk Register - Approved Mitigation Plans - Escalation Reports to Governance Committee - Residual Risk Dashboard ## Key Decisions - Risk priority ranking based on likelihood and impact scores - Approval or rejection of proposed mitigation strategies - Escalation decision when residual risk exceeds appetite threshold ## Exception Paths - If risk assessment identifies a critical risk, it is immediately escalated to the Risk Committee without waiting for the full assessment cycle - If a mitigation plan is rejected, the Risk Officer must rework the plan and resubmit - If residual risk remains above appetite after mitigation, an escalation report is sent to the Executive Sponsor ## Step Walkthrough 1. Collect Risk Data: The Risk Officer collects all relevant data sources including the latest internal audit findings, external environment scan reports, and current strategic plan objectives. This data forms the foundation for identifying new and emerging risks. The officer also reviews the previous risk register for any carry-over items. 2. Assess Risks: The Risk Officer assesses each identified risk using a standard likelihood and impact matrix. Likelihood is rated from Rare to Almost Certain, and impact from Insignificant to Catastrophic. The combined score determines the risk rating (Low, Medium, High, Critical). This step produces a scored risk list. 3. Prioritize Risks: The system automatically sorts all scored risks by their rating, with Critical risks at the top. It compares each risk score against the institutional risk appetite statement. Any risk rated High or Critical is flagged for mandatory committee review. The output is a prioritized risk register. 4. Develop Mitigation Plans: For each risk rated High or Critical, the Risk Officer develops a detailed mitigation plan. Each plan includes specific actions to reduce likelihood or impact, assigned owners, target completion dates, and expected residual risk level. Plans are documented in the risk register. 5. Review Mitigation Plans: The Risk Committee meets to review each draft mitigation plan. They evaluate the proposed actions for feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and alignment with risk appetite. The committee either approves the plan for implementation or rejects it with comments for rework. 6. Implement Mitigations: The Risk Officer executes the approved mitigation actions, coordinating with assigned owners. Each action is tracked to completion, and the risk register is updated with implementation status, actual residual risk, and any lessons learned. 7. Monitor Residual Risk: The system continuously monitors the residual risk levels for all implemented mitigations. It compares actual residual risk against the institutional risk appetite. If any risk remains above the acceptable threshold, an automatic alert is generated for the Risk Officer. 8. Escalate Exceptions: The Risk Officer compiles an escalation report detailing the risk, the mitigation actions taken, and the residual risk level that remains above appetite. This report is sent to the Executive Sponsor and the Governance Committee for further decision-making.
