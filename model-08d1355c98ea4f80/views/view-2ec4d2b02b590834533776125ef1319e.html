Business Continuity Mgt ()
## Purpose Business Continuity Management ensures the institution can maintain or rapidly resume critical academic and administrative operations during and after a disruption. It systematically identifies essential functions, assesses risks and impacts, and develops actionable plans that protect people, assets, and reputation. The process embeds resilience into the institution's operational fabric, enabling uninterrupted student services and research continuity. ## Roles - **Business Continuity Manager**: Leads the development, maintenance, and testing of continuity plans; coordinates the BIA process. - **Department Heads / Function Owners**: Identify critical functions, provide input on dependencies and resource needs, and participate in testing. - **Risk Management Team**: Provides risk assessment data and threat intelligence. - **IT / Infrastructure Team**: Supplies resource availability data and supports technical recovery planning. - **Executive Governance Committee**: Reviews and approves continuity plans and major strategy decisions. - **System**: Automated notification and document management systems. ## Upstream Inputs - Risk Assessment Reports (from Risk Management) - Institutional Strategic Priorities (from Strategy) - Resource Availability Data (from IT, Facilities, HR) - Regulatory and Compliance Requirements - Incident History and Lessons Learned ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Business Continuity Plans (documented and published) - Business Impact Analysis (BIA) Reports - Test and Exercise Schedules and Results - Plan Update Logs and Version History - Incident Response Activation Protocols ## Key Decisions - Determination of critical vs. non-critical business functions during BIA - Selection of appropriate continuity strategies (e.g., alternate site, remote work, manual procedures) - Approval of recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO) - Acceptance of residual risk where full recovery is not feasible - Decision to activate a plan during an actual incident ## Exception Paths - **BIA Data Incomplete**: If function owners fail to provide adequate input, the BIA step loops back for data collection with escalation to department heads. - **Plan Review Rejected**: If the governance committee rejects a plan, it is returned to the Business Continuity Manager for revision with documented feedback. - **Test Failure / Gaps Found**: If a test reveals critical gaps, a dedicated remediation step (Plan Remediation) is triggered before re-testing. - **Resource Constraints**: If required resources are unavailable, an exception path escalates to executive leadership for prioritization decisions. ## Step Walkthrough 1. **Gather Input Data**: The Business Continuity Manager collects risk assessments, institutional priorities, and resource availability data from upstream sources. 2. **Conduct Business Impact Analysis**: Function owners and the BCM team analyze critical functions, dependencies, and recovery requirements, producing a BIA report. 3. **Develop Continuity Strategies**: Based on the BIA, the team formulates recovery strategies for each critical function, considering alternate work arrangements, technology failover, and manual workarounds. 4. **Draft Continuity Plans**: The Business Continuity Manager documents the strategies into formal, structured business continuity plans with roles, procedures, and contact lists. 5. **Review and Approve Plans**: The Executive Governance Committee reviews the draft plans; if approved, they move forward; if rejected, they are returned for revision. 6. **Communicate and Train**: Approved plans are distributed to all relevant stakeholders, and training sessions are conducted to ensure awareness and readiness. 7. **Test and Exercise Plans**: The team conducts scheduled tests (tabletop, walkthrough, or full simulation) to validate plan effectiveness and identify gaps. 8. **Review Test Results**: Test outcomes are analyzed; if gaps are found, a remediation step is triggered; if successful, the plan is confirmed as effective. 9. **Remediate Plan Gaps**: The Business Continuity Manager addresses identified issues, updates the plan, and re-enters the testing cycle. 10. **Update and Maintain Plans**: Plans are periodically reviewed and updated based on test results, organizational changes, and evolving threats, ensuring continuous improvement. Steps: 1. Gather Input Data: The Business Continuity Manager initiates the process by gathering all necessary upstream inputs including risk assessment reports from the Risk Management team, institutional strategic priorities from the Strategy function, and resource availability data from IT, Facilities, and HR. This data provides the foundation for the subsequent analysis and planning activities. 2. Conduct Business Impact Analysis: The Business Continuity Manager facilitates a structured Business Impact Analysis (BIA) with department heads and function owners. They identify critical business functions, assess the impact of disruptions over time, determine dependencies (people, technology, facilities), and establish recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO). The output is a formal BIA report. 3. Develop Continuity Strategies: Using the BIA report, the Business Continuity Manager and function owners develop appropriate continuity strategies for each critical function. Strategies may include alternate work locations, remote work enablement, technology failover, manual workarounds, cross-training staff, or outsourcing. Each strategy is evaluated for feasibility, cost, and alignment with institutional priorities. 4. Draft Continuity Plans: The Business Continuity Manager consolidates the approved strategies into comprehensive, structured business continuity plans. Each plan includes activation criteria, roles and responsibilities, step-by-step procedures, communication protocols, contact lists, resource inventories, and escalation paths. Plans are version-controlled and stored in the BCM document repository. 5. Review and Approve Plans: The Executive Governance Committee reviews the draft business continuity plans for completeness, feasibility, alignment with institutional risk appetite, and regulatory compliance. The committee may approve the plans, request revisions with specific feedback, or reject them if fundamental issues are identified. Approval authorizes the plans for implementation. 6. Communicate and Train: The Business Continuity Manager disseminates the approved plans to all relevant stakeholders, including department heads, incident response teams, and key personnel. Training sessions are conducted to ensure everyone understands their roles, procedures, and communication channels. Training records are maintained for audit purposes. 7. Test and Exercise Plans: The Business Continuity Manager schedules and facilitates regular tests and exercises of the continuity plans. Tests may range from simple tabletop discussions to full-scale simulations involving multiple departments. The objective is to validate the plan's operational effectiveness, identify gaps, and build muscle memory among responders. 8. Review Test Results: The Business Continuity Manager and relevant stakeholders review the test results and observations. They assess whether the plan performed as expected, identify any gaps, failures, or areas for improvement, and document lessons learned. This analysis determines whether the plan is effective or requires remediation. 9. Remediate Plan Gaps: If gaps or failures were identified during testing, the Business Continuity Manager leads a remediation effort. This involves root cause analysis, developing corrective actions, updating plan procedures, re-allocating resources, or revising strategies. The updated plan is then re-entered into the testing cycle to confirm effectiveness. 10. Update and Maintain Plans: The Business Continuity Manager conducts periodic reviews of all continuity plans, incorporating lessons learned from tests, actual incidents, organizational changes, regulatory updates, and evolving threats. Plans are updated, re-approved if necessary, and re-communicated to stakeholders. This ensures the plans remain current and effective over time.
