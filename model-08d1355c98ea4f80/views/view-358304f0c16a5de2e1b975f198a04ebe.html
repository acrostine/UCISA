Research Prioritisation ()
## Purpose This process systematically ranks and selects research opportunities to ensure the institution invests in projects that offer the highest strategic value, academic impact, and funding potential. It balances ambition with resource constraints to produce a defensible, transparent portfolio. ## Roles - Research Strategy Lead: Facilitates the evaluation and ranking sessions. - Department Heads: Provide stakeholder input and strategic direction. - Resource Analyst: Supplies resource forecasts and capacity data. - Portfolio Manager: Compiles and publishes the final prioritized list. - Dean of Research: Approves the final portfolio. ## Upstream Inputs - Analysis Reports: Opportunity analysis and feasibility studies. - Stakeholder Inputs: Strategic priorities from departments and leadership. - Resource Forecasts: Staff, equipment, and budget availability data. ## Downstream Outputs - Prioritised Research Portfolio: Ranked list of selected projects. - Resource Allocation Plan: Mapping of resources to approved projects. - Rejected Opportunities Log: Record of opportunities not selected. ## Key Decisions - Strategic alignment score: Does the opportunity meet institutional research goals? - Impact threshold: Does the opportunity exceed the minimum impact score? - Resource feasibility: Are sufficient resources available within the planning horizon? - Portfolio balance: Does the selection maintain a healthy mix of disciplines and risk levels? ## Exception Paths - If an opportunity fails strategic alignment, it is rejected and logged. - If an opportunity fails the impact threshold, it is rejected and logged. - If resources are insufficient, the opportunity is flagged for deferral or rejection. - If the Dean does not approve the portfolio, it is returned for rework. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Compile Opportunity List: The Research Strategy Lead collects analysis reports and stakeholder inputs to create a comprehensive list of all research opportunities under consideration. This list forms the baseline for all subsequent evaluation steps. 2. Assess Strategic Alignment: The Research Strategy Lead scores each opportunity on how well it aligns with the institution's research strategy, mission, and long-term objectives. Opportunities that do not meet the minimum alignment threshold are rejected. 3. Evaluate Potential Impact: Department Heads and the Research Strategy Lead jointly score each opportunity for its potential academic contribution, societal benefit, and likelihood of attracting funding. Opportunities below the impact threshold are rejected. 4. Review Resource Feasibility: The Resource Analyst reviews resource forecasts and maps them against the requirements of each opportunity. Opportunities that cannot be resourced within the planning horizon are flagged for deferral or rejection. 5. Rank Opportunities: The Portfolio Manager runs a ranking algorithm that combines strategic alignment, impact scores, and resource feasibility into a single priority score. The result is a preliminary ranked list of opportunities. 6. Review Portfolio Balance: The Research Strategy Lead and Department Heads review the preliminary ranked list to ensure it includes a healthy mix of disciplines, risk levels, and strategic priorities. Adjustments are made if the portfolio is unbalanced. 7. Approve Final Portfolio: The Dean of Research reviews the balanced portfolio draft. If approved, it becomes the final prioritized portfolio. If not, it is returned to the Research Strategy Lead for rework. 8. Publish Prioritised Portfolio: The Portfolio Manager publishes the approved portfolio to the research management system and notifies all stakeholders. The rejected opportunities log is archived for future reference. 9. Rework Portfolio Draft: The Research Strategy Lead revises the balanced portfolio draft to address the Dean's concerns, then resubmits it for approval.
