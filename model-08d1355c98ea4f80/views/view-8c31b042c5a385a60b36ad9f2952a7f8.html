Internal Audit & Reporting ()
## Purpose This process provides an independent, objective assessment of the institution's internal controls, risk management practices, and governance processes. It ensures that control weaknesses and compliance gaps are identified, reported, and remediated in a timely manner, thereby strengthening institutional integrity and accountability. ## Roles - Chief Audit Executive (CAE): Oversees the audit function, approves the audit scope, and presents final reports to the audit committee. - Lead Auditor: Plans and executes the audit engagement, supervises the audit team, and drafts the audit report. - Staff Auditor: Performs detailed testing, documents evidence, and supports report preparation. - Process Owner (Auditee): Provides access to data and personnel, responds to preliminary findings, and implements corrective actions. - Audit Committee: Reviews final audit reports and monitors management's response to recommendations. - System: Automated audit management system used for workpaper storage, issue tracking, and report generation. ## Upstream Inputs - Annual Audit Plan - Audit Request or Trigger - Operational Data (transaction logs, process documentation) - Compliance Records (regulatory filings, policy attestations) - Governance Frameworks (policies, charters, risk appetite statements) - Prior Audit Reports and Remediation Status ## Downstream Outputs - Audit Engagement Letter - Audit Workpapers and Evidence - Preliminary Findings Summary - Final Audit Report - Management Action Plan - Remediation Status Report ## Key Decisions - Determine audit scope and objectives based on risk assessment and audit plan. - Evaluate whether a finding constitutes a material weakness, significant deficiency, or observation. - Decide on the severity and priority of each finding for reporting and remediation. - Approve the final audit report for distribution to the audit committee. ## Exception Paths - If critical control failures are identified during fieldwork, an interim flash report is issued to senior management immediately. - If process owners dispute findings, a formal escalation and mediation process is triggered with the CAE. - If remediation actions are not completed by the due date, the issue is escalated to the audit committee and added to the risk register. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Audit Request: The audit request is received via the audit management system or directly from the audit committee. The lead auditor reviews the request against the annual audit plan, confirms the engagement with the process owner, and initiates the audit lifecycle. This step ensures alignment with strategic priorities and resource availability. 2. Scope Audit Engagement: The lead auditor conducts a preliminary risk assessment to refine the audit scope. Objectives, criteria, timelines, and resource needs are documented in an engagement letter. The letter is sent to the process owner for acknowledgment. This step ensures a clear mutual understanding of the audit's boundaries and deliverables. 3. Gather Operational Data: Staff auditors request and retrieve relevant data from the process owner and system repositories. This includes transaction logs, policy documents, prior audit reports, and compliance filings. Data is organized and stored in the audit workpaper repository for subsequent testing. 4. Perform Control Testing: Staff auditors perform walkthroughs, sample testing, and data analytics to evaluate control design and operating effectiveness. Test results are documented in workpapers with clear evidence. Any deviations or exceptions are flagged for further evaluation. 5. Evaluate Findings: The lead auditor reviews all test exceptions and deviations. Each finding is evaluated against audit criteria and classified as a material weakness, significant deficiency, or observation. Root cause analysis is performed to inform recommendations. This step determines the content and tone of the final report. 6. Review Findings with Process Owner: A meeting is held with the process owner to discuss each finding. The process owner may provide additional context, dispute the finding, or agree and propose corrective actions. The lead auditor updates the findings based on the discussion and documents the process owner's response. 7. Draft Audit Report: The lead auditor synthesizes all findings, risk ratings, root causes, and management responses into a formal draft audit report. Recommendations are prioritized and linked to specific remediation actions. The draft is reviewed internally by the audit team for quality and completeness. 8. Approve Audit Report: The Chief Audit Executive reviews the draft report for quality, objectivity, and alignment with audit standards. Any necessary revisions are requested. Once satisfied, the CAE formally approves the report for issuance to the audit committee and senior leadership. 9. Issue Audit Report: The approved audit report is distributed to the audit committee, senior leadership, and relevant stakeholders. A management action plan template is included, requesting formal responses and remediation timelines for each finding. The report is archived in the audit management system. 10. Track Remediation: The audit team tracks each remediation action against its due date. Status updates are collected from process owners quarterly. Overdue or incomplete actions are escalated to the audit committee. The remediation status report is updated and maintained in the audit management system.
