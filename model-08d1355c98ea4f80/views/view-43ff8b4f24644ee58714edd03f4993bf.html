Accommodation Departure & Turnaround Mgt ()
## Purpose Accommodation Departure & Turnaround Management ensures that when an occupant departs, the room is systematically cleaned, repaired, and inspected so it can be re-allocated with minimal delay. This process maintains living standards and maximises accommodation asset utilisation. ## Roles - Accommodation Officer: Initiates departure, schedules cleaning and maintenance, coordinates inspections - Maintenance Technician: Performs repairs and maintenance tasks - Cleaning Staff: Cleans the vacated room - Quality Inspector: Conducts final inspection and certifies room readiness - System: Automates notifications, status updates, and record keeping ## Upstream Inputs - Departure Notice - Room Condition Report (pre-departure) - Maintenance Schedule - Occupant Exit Confirmation ## Downstream Outputs - Ready-to-Occupy Accommodation Unit - Completed Inspection Report - Updated Room Status (in system) - Maintenance Work Order (if required) ## Key Decisions - Does the room require maintenance beyond standard cleaning? - Does the room pass the final quality inspection? - Is the room ready for immediate re-allocation? ## Exception Paths - If damage or maintenance issues are found during initial inspection, a maintenance work order is raised and the room is held until repairs are completed. - If the final inspection fails, the room is sent back for re-cleaning or rework before re-inspection. - If occupant does not vacate on time, escalation to supervisor occurs (out of scope for this process). ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Departure Notice: The Accommodation Officer receives a departure notice from the occupant or housing system. The notice is logged into the accommodation management system, triggering the turnaround workflow. The room is flagged as pending vacation. 2. Conduct Exit Inspection: After the occupant has vacated, the Accommodation Officer performs a walkthrough inspection of the room. The condition of fixtures, furniture, and fittings is recorded in an inspection report. Any damage, excessive wear, or missing items are noted. 3. Assess Maintenance Needs: The Accommodation Officer reviews the exit inspection report to assess whether any maintenance or repairs are needed. If damage or defects are found, a maintenance work order is raised. If the room is in good condition, only standard cleaning is required. 4. Schedule Cleaning: The Accommodation Officer creates a cleaning task in the system and assigns it to available cleaning staff. The task includes the room number, required cleaning standards, and any special instructions based on the inspection report. 5. Schedule Maintenance: If a maintenance work order was raised, the Accommodation Officer assigns a Maintenance Technician to complete the repairs. The work order specifies the scope of work, priority, and deadline. The technician acknowledges the task. 6. Perform Cleaning: Cleaning Staff attends the vacated room and performs a thorough clean including floors, surfaces, windows, bathroom, and kitchenette. The cleaning is completed according to the defined standards. The staff member marks the cleaning task as complete in the system. 7. Perform Maintenance: The Maintenance Technician performs the required repairs or replacements as specified in the work order. This may include fixing fixtures, painting, repairing appliances, or other maintenance tasks. Upon completion, the technician updates the work order status to 'Completed'. 8. Conduct Final Inspection: The Quality Inspector performs a final inspection of the room to verify that cleaning and maintenance have been completed to the required standard. The inspector checks all areas and systems. A pass or fail decision is recorded in the inspection report. 9. Update Room Status: Upon receiving a passed final inspection, the Accommodation Officer updates the room status in the system to 'Ready-to-Occupy'. The system automatically notifies the allocation team that the unit is available for re-allocation. All records are archived.
