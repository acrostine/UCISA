Change Portfolio Mgt ()
## Purpose This process ensures that the institution's change initiatives are systematically evaluated, prioritised, and balanced to maximise strategic value while managing resource constraints and risk. It transforms raw project proposals into an approved, coherent portfolio plan that aligns with the university's transformation goals. ## Roles - Portfolio Manager: Facilitates evaluation, prioritisation, and plan creation. - Business Sponsor: Submits and champions change proposals. - Resource Manager: Provides capacity data and constraints. - Governance Committee: Reviews and approves the final portfolio plan. - System: Automated scoring and capacity modelling. ## Upstream Inputs - Project Proposals - Strategic Priorities - Resource Availability Data - Risk Register ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Change Portfolio Plan - Prioritised Initiative List - Resource Allocation Summary - Portfolio Risk Report ## Key Decisions - Which proposals meet minimum strategic alignment threshold? - How to rank competing proposals when resources are constrained? - What is the acceptable portfolio risk level? - Which initiatives are deferred or rejected? ## Exception Paths - Proposal fails strategic alignment check: returned to sponsor with feedback. - Resource capacity exceeded: portfolio rebalanced or initiatives deferred. - Governance committee rejects plan: revision and resubmission required. - Critical risk identified: initiative flagged for mitigation or removal. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Collect Proposals: The Portfolio Manager issues a call for change proposals and collects submissions from Business Sponsors across the institution. Each proposal includes scope, estimated cost, resource needs, expected benefits, and alignment to strategic objectives. All proposals are logged into the portfolio management system. 2. Assess Strategic Alignment: The system automatically evaluates each proposal against the current strategic priorities using a weighted scoring model. Proposals that fall below a minimum alignment threshold are flagged for rejection. A strategic alignment score is generated for every proposal. 3. Evaluate Value and Risk: The Portfolio Manager reviews each proposal's expected benefits, implementation cost, and associated risks. A combined value-risk score is assigned. Proposals with unacceptable risk profiles are flagged for further review or rejection. 4. Prioritise Initiatives: The Portfolio Manager ranks all proposals based on a weighted combination of strategic alignment, value, and risk scores. A prioritised list is created, with top-ranked initiatives recommended for inclusion in the portfolio. 5. Model Resource Capacity: The system models the resource demand of prioritised initiatives against available capacity data from Resource Managers. Capacity gaps and constraints are identified and visualised. A capacity summary report is generated. 6. Balance Portfolio: The Portfolio Manager reviews capacity constraints and adjusts initiative sequencing, defers lower-priority items, or removes proposals that cannot be resourced. The result is a balanced draft portfolio plan that fits within available capacity. 7. Review Portfolio Plan: The Governance Committee reviews the draft portfolio plan, including prioritisation rationale, resource allocation, and risk profile. The committee may approve the plan, request revisions, or reject it. Approval authorises execution. 8. Finalise and Communicate: The Portfolio Manager publishes the final approved portfolio plan, updates the portfolio management system, and communicates the plan to all stakeholders including Business Sponsors, Resource Managers, and project teams.
