## Purpose Digital Communication Management ensures that all outbound digital messaging from the institution is created, reviewed, approved, and distributed in a controlled and consistent manner. It aligns communications with strategic objectives, maintains brand integrity, and maximizes stakeholder engagement through appropriate channel selection. ## Roles - Communication Manager: Oversees the process, approves final content, and selects distribution channels. - Content Author: Drafts the initial communication content based on provided objectives. - Brand Reviewer: Checks content for brand consistency and policy compliance. - System: Automates distribution and performance data collection. ## Upstream Inputs - Communication Objectives: Strategic goals and messaging priorities. - Content Inputs: Raw text, images, links, and other assets for the communication. ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Communication: Finalized, brand-compliant message ready for distribution. - Distributed Message: Communication sent via selected channels. - Performance Report: Engagement metrics and feedback data. ## Key Decisions - Channel Selection: Which digital channels (email, portal, social media, SMS) are most effective for the target audience. - Content Approval: Whether the draft meets brand and policy standards. - Distribution Timing: When to send the communication for maximum impact. ## Exception Paths - Brand Non-Compliance: Content fails brand review; sent back for revision. - Distribution Failure: Channel error prevents delivery; communication is queued for retry or alternative channel. - Low Engagement: Post-distribution metrics fall below threshold; triggers a review and adjustment for future communications. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Communication Request: The process begins when an internal stakeholder submits a communication request. This includes the communication objectives, target audience, key messages, and any supporting content assets. The request is logged into the system for tracking. 2. Draft Communication Content: The Content Author uses the communication objectives and content inputs to write the first draft. This includes text, images, links, and any calls to action. The draft is formatted for the intended channels. 3. Select Distribution Channels: The Communication Manager evaluates the target audience, message urgency, and content format to select the most effective digital channels. Options include email, institutional portal, social media, SMS, and digital signage. 4. Review Brand Compliance: The Brand Reviewer examines the content draft against institutional brand guidelines, accessibility standards, and communication policies. They verify logo usage, tone, messaging consistency, and legal disclaimers. 5. Approve Communication: The Communication Manager reviews the brand-compliant draft and the selected channels. They make the final decision to approve or reject the communication. If approved, it moves to distribution. 6. Distribute Message: The System automatically distributes the approved communication to the selected channels at the scheduled time. It handles formatting, personalization, and delivery tracking for each channel. 7. Collect Performance Data: After distribution, the System collects performance data such as open rates, click-through rates, engagement time, and any direct feedback. This data is compiled into a report. 8. Archive Communication Record: The System archives the approved communication, distribution details, and performance report for audit, compliance, and future reference. The record is indexed for easy retrieval.
