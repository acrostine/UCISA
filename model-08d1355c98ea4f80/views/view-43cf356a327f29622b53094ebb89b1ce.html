Asset Mgt ()
## Purpose This process ensures that all institutional assets are accurately recorded, tracked from acquisition through disposal, and valued correctly for financial statements and compliance. It provides a single source of truth for asset information and supports audit and regulatory requirements. ## Roles - Asset Manager: Responsible for creating and maintaining asset records, initiating disposal, and approving valuations. - Finance Officer: Calculates and applies depreciation, reviews valuation reports. - Verifier: Conducts physical verification of assets. - System: Automates depreciation calculations and generates reports. ## Upstream Inputs - Asset Acquisition Data: Details of newly purchased or acquired assets including cost, date, category, and location. - Disposal Request: Notification that an asset is to be disposed of. ## Downstream Outputs - Asset Register: Complete, up-to-date list of all institutional assets with status and location. - Valuation Report: Depreciation schedule and current book value of assets for financial statements. - Disposal Record: Documentation of asset disposal for audit trail. ## Key Decisions - Asset Category Assignment: Determining the correct asset category for depreciation rules. - Depreciation Method: Selecting straight-line or reducing balance method based on asset type. - Disposal Approval: Whether to approve or reject a disposal request based on asset condition and residual value. - Valuation Review: Confirming that calculated values are reasonable and accurate. ## Exception Paths - Asset Not Found During Verification: Asset record is flagged, investigation initiated, and record updated or corrected. - Disposal Request Rejected: Asset is retained, and a note is added to the record explaining the decision. - Valuation Discrepancy: If calculated depreciation appears incorrect, the Finance Officer reviews and recalculates. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Asset Data: The process begins when asset acquisition data is received from the procurement system or manually entered. This data includes asset name, purchase cost, acquisition date, category, location, and any warranty information. The data is logged into the asset management system for processing. 2. Create Asset Record: The Asset Manager uses the acquisition data to create a new asset record in the asset register. They verify all fields are correct, assign a unique asset ID, and add any additional notes. The record is saved and becomes the official entry for the asset. 3. Verify Asset Existence: The Verifier visits the asset location and physically inspects the asset. They confirm the asset matches the record details including serial number, condition, and location. A verification report is completed and attached to the asset record. 4. Assign Depreciation Rules: The Finance Officer reviews the asset category and selects the appropriate depreciation method (straight-line or reducing balance) and useful life. These rules are applied to the asset record and will drive future depreciation calculations. 5. Calculate Depreciation: The system uses the assigned depreciation rules to calculate the periodic depreciation amount. This calculation is performed at the end of each reporting period. The result is stored and applied to the asset's book value. 6. Review Valuation Report: The Finance Officer reviews the valuation report generated by the system. They check that depreciation amounts are reasonable, asset values are accurate, and no anomalies exist. If discrepancies are found, they can recalculate or adjust the depreciation rules. 7. Initiate Disposal: When an asset reaches end of life or is no longer needed, the Asset Manager initiates a disposal request. They provide the reason for disposal, estimated residual value, and any supporting documentation. The request is submitted for approval. 8. Approve Disposal: The Asset Manager reviews the disposal request, considering asset condition, residual value, and business need. They either approve the disposal, allowing the asset to be removed, or reject it, retaining the asset in the register with a note. 9. Record Disposal: Upon approval, the system records the disposal by updating the asset register. The asset is marked as disposed, the disposal date and method are recorded, and the asset is removed from active depreciation calculations. The disposal record is stored for audit purposes. 10. Resolve Discrepancy: If the valuation report review reveals discrepancies, the Finance Officer investigates the cause. They may adjust depreciation rules, correct data entry errors, or recalculate values. Once resolved, the valuation report is regenerated and reviewed again. 11. Retain Asset: When a disposal request is rejected, the Asset Manager adds a note to the asset record explaining the reason for retention. The asset remains active in the register and continues to be depreciated normally.
