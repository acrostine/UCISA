## Purpose Research Output Reporting compiles data on scholarly publications and research outputs generated by academic staff. It consumes publication records, citation metrics, and funding data to produce comprehensive reports for internal assessment, funding bodies, and external rankings. This process supports transparency, compliance with funding requirements, and enhances institutional reputation by demonstrating research impact. ## Roles - Research Data Coordinator: Collects and validates publication data - Research Office Manager: Reviews and approves reports - System: Automates data retrieval and report generation ## Upstream Inputs - Publication Records from institutional repository - Citation Metrics from Scopus/Web of Science - Funding Data from research grants system ## Downstream Outputs - Internal Research Performance Report - Funding Body Compliance Report - External Ranking Submission ## Key Decisions - Determine if publication records are complete and accurate - Decide whether to include or exclude specific outputs based on quality criteria - Approve final report for distribution ## Exception Paths - Missing or incomplete publication records trigger a data collection rework loop - Citation data discrepancies require manual verification and correction - Funding data mismatches are escalated to the grants office ## Step Walkthrough 1. Collect Publication Records: The Research Data Coordinator initiates the process by extracting publication records from the institutional repository and supplementing them with data from external sources such as Scopus and Web of Science. This step ensures all scholarly outputs are captured for reporting. 2. Validate Data Completeness: The system automatically validates each record against a completeness checklist, including required fields like author names, publication year, DOI, and journal title. Incomplete records are flagged for manual review. 3. Enrich with Citation Metrics: The system queries external citation databases to retrieve citation counts, h-index, and journal impact factors for each publication. This data is appended to the records to support impact analysis. 4. Integrate Funding Data: The Research Data Coordinator matches each publication with corresponding funding data from the research grants system. This ensures compliance with funder reporting requirements. 5. Compile Draft Report: The system compiles the complete publication dataset into a draft report formatted for internal assessment, funding bodies, or external rankings. The report includes summary statistics and detailed listings. 6. Review Draft Report: The Research Office Manager examines the draft report to verify data accuracy, check for anomalies, and ensure compliance with reporting standards. Any issues are noted for correction. 7. Approve Final Report: The Research Office Manager makes the final decision to approve the report for distribution or reject it with feedback for rework. Approval confirms the report meets all quality and compliance standards. 8. Distribute Report: The system distributes the approved report via email or secure portal to designated recipients, including internal research committees, funding agencies, and ranking organizations. A distribution log is maintained for audit purposes. 9. Correct Incomplete Records: The Research Data Coordinator reviews flagged incomplete records and corrects missing or erroneous fields by consulting authors or external sources. Corrected records are resubmitted for validation. 10. Address Review Feedback: The Research Data Coordinator implements changes to the draft report as directed by the Research Office Manager's feedback. Corrections may involve data updates, formatting adjustments, or additional verification.
