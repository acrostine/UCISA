Teaching & Learning Content Mgt ()
## Purpose Teaching & Learning Content Management ensures that all educational materials used in course delivery are developed, curated, reviewed, and published in a controlled manner. This process maintains content quality, curriculum alignment, and currency, directly supporting effective teaching and learning outcomes. ## Roles - **Content Developer**: Creates or curates new learning materials based on academic input. - **Academic Reviewer**: Reviews content for curriculum alignment, accuracy, and pedagogical soundness. - **Quality Assurance Officer**: Performs final compliance and quality checks before publication. - **System Administrator**: Publishes approved content to the learning management system. ## Upstream Inputs - Content Request from academic staff - Curriculum Framework documents - Existing content repository assets - Academic feedback and improvement suggestions ## Downstream Outputs - Published learning materials in the LMS - Content update notifications to teaching staff - Archived previous versions of content ## Key Decisions - Whether developed content meets curriculum alignment standards - Whether content passes quality assurance checks - Whether content requires rework or is ready for publication ## Exception Paths - Content fails academic review: sent back to Content Developer for rework with specific feedback - Content fails quality assurance: sent back to Content Developer for correction before re-review - Content request deemed out of scope: rejected with explanation to requester ## Step Walkthrough 1. **Receive Content Request**: Content Developer receives and logs a new content request from academic staff, capturing scope and requirements. 2. **Develop or Curate Content**: Content Developer creates new materials or curates existing resources, ensuring alignment with the curriculum framework. 3. **Review Content for Alignment**: Academic Reviewer evaluates the content against curriculum standards and pedagogical requirements, producing an approval or rejection decision. 4. **Rework Content**: If rejected, Content Developer revises the content based on reviewer feedback and resubmits for review. 5. **Perform Quality Assurance**: Quality Assurance Officer checks the approved content for formatting, accessibility, and compliance standards, producing a pass or fail decision. 6. **Correct Content Issues**: If QA fails, Content Developer fixes identified issues and returns the content for re-review. 7. **Publish Content to LMS**: System Administrator publishes the final approved content to the learning management system and notifies relevant teaching staff. Steps: 1. Receive Content Request: The Content Developer receives a content request from an academic staff member via the content management system. The request is logged with details including course code, content type, required delivery date, and any specific curriculum references. The developer validates that the request is complete and within scope before proceeding. 2. Develop or Curate Content: Based on the validated request, the Content Developer either creates new educational materials or curates existing resources from the content repository. The developer ensures all materials align with the current curriculum framework, learning objectives, and pedagogical best practices. Draft content is saved in the working area of the content management system. 3. Review Content for Alignment: The Academic Reviewer examines the draft content for accuracy, curriculum alignment, and pedagogical soundness. The reviewer checks that learning objectives are met, content is factually correct, and the material is appropriate for the target student cohort. A decision is made to either approve the content or return it with revision requests. 4. Rework Content: The Content Developer receives the review feedback and revises the content accordingly. All reviewer comments are addressed, and the content is updated in the working area. Once revisions are complete, the content is resubmitted for another academic review cycle. 5. Perform Quality Assurance: The Quality Assurance Officer reviews the approved content for formatting consistency, accessibility standards (e.g., WCAG), branding guidelines, and technical compliance. The officer verifies that all multimedia elements function correctly and that the content is ready for publication. A pass or fail decision is recorded. 6. Correct Content Issues: The Content Developer receives the QA issues list and corrects each identified problem. This may involve reformatting documents, adding alternative text to images, or fixing broken links. Once all issues are resolved, the content is resubmitted for QA re-review. 7. Publish Content to LMS: The System Administrator takes the final approved and QA-passed content and publishes it to the production learning management system. The content is assigned to the correct course modules, versioned, and made visible to students and teaching staff. Notifications are sent to relevant stakeholders about the content update.
