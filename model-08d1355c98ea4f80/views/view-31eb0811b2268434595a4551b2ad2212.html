## Purpose Organisational Design ensures the enterprise structure, roles, and governance frameworks are purpose-built to execute strategic goals and deliver operational efficiency. It synthesises workforce analytics, strategic direction, and stakeholder perspectives into a coherent model that clarifies accountability and supports the institution's evolving needs. ## Roles - Chief Strategy Officer: Approves the final organisational design and governance framework - HR Business Partner: Provides workforce data and role design expertise - Organisational Design Lead: Facilitates the design process and produces deliverables - Stakeholder Group: Reviews and provides input on proposed structures - System: Generates organisational charts and publishes role definitions ## Upstream Inputs - Strategic Plan - Workforce Data (headcount, skills, demographics) - Stakeholder Input (interviews, surveys, workshop outcomes) - Current Organisational Chart - Governance Framework Requirements ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Organisational Chart - Role Definitions and Accountability Matrix - Governance Framework Document - Implementation Roadmap ## Key Decisions - Whether the proposed structure sufficiently supports strategic objectives - Whether role definitions are clear and non-overlapping - Whether the governance framework provides adequate oversight without creating bottlenecks - Whether to approve the design as-is or require revisions ## Exception Paths - If stakeholder feedback reveals significant gaps, the design is sent back for rework - If the approval gate rejects the design, a dedicated remediation step addresses findings before resubmission - If workforce data is incomplete, the process pauses until data is obtained ## Step Walkthrough 1. Analyse Current State: The Organisational Design Lead gathers the strategic plan, workforce data, and current organisational chart. They conduct a gap analysis comparing current capabilities against strategic objectives, identifying structural inefficiencies, role overlaps, and missing governance elements. This analysis forms the evidence base for the target design. 2. Design Target Structure: Based on the current state analysis, the Organisational Design Lead drafts a target organisational structure. This includes defining new or revised roles, establishing reporting lines, and outlining a governance framework that specifies decision rights, committees, and escalation paths. The draft is documented in a structured design proposal. 3. Validate with Stakeholders: The Organisational Design Lead facilitates workshops and review sessions with the Stakeholder Group. They present the draft structure, role definitions, and governance model, then capture feedback on clarity, feasibility, and alignment with operational needs. Feedback is recorded in a structured format for analysis. 4. Assess Feedback and Refine: The Organisational Design Lead reviews the stakeholder feedback log, categorising comments into structural changes, role clarifications, and governance adjustments. They update the draft design proposal accordingly, ensuring all material concerns are addressed while maintaining strategic alignment. The refined design is prepared for formal approval. 5. Approve Design: The Chief Strategy Officer examines the refined organisational design proposal against strategic objectives, workforce constraints, and governance requirements. They make a formal decision to approve the design for implementation or to reject it with specific findings that require remediation. 6. Address Approval Findings: If the Chief Strategy Officer rejects the design, the Organisational Design Lead reviews the documented findings and makes targeted revisions to the proposal. This may involve restructuring reporting lines, clarifying role accountabilities, or adjusting governance mechanisms. The revised proposal is resubmitted for approval. 7. Generate Organisational Charts: Upon approval, the system automatically generates organisational charts in standard formats (Visio, PDF, web-based). It also produces a role definition document listing each role's title, reporting line, key responsibilities, and required competencies. These artefacts are version-controlled and stored in the enterprise repository. 8. Publish Role Definitions: The HR Business Partner reviews the generated role definitions for accuracy and completeness, then publishes them to the enterprise HR system and the organisational knowledge base. The accountability matrix is distributed to all relevant stakeholders, and the implementation roadmap is updated with the new structure.
