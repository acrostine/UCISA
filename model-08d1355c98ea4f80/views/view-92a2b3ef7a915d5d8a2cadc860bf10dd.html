Mail Mgt ()
## Purpose Mail Management ensures that all physical correspondence and parcels entering or leaving the institution are handled efficiently, securely, and in compliance with service agreements. This process supports effective communication channels critical to administrative and academic operations by providing a structured workflow for receipt, sorting, distribution, and dispatch. ## Roles - Mail Clerk: Receives, sorts, and distributes incoming mail; prepares outgoing mail for dispatch. - Mail Supervisor: Reviews exception items, verifies high-value or sensitive parcels, and approves dispatch. - System: Automated logging, tracking updates, and service agreement validation. ## Upstream Inputs - Incoming Mail and Parcels from postal carriers - Outgoing Mail Collection from internal departments - Service Agreements with postal and courier providers - Recipient Directory (staff/student location data) ## Downstream Outputs - Delivered Mail to internal recipients - Dispatched Outgoing Mail to carriers - Mail Tracking Records (logged in system) - Exception Reports (undeliverable or suspicious items) ## Key Decisions - Is the incoming item suspicious or high-value requiring supervisor review? - Is the recipient address valid and current in the directory? - Does the outgoing parcel meet service agreement weight/size limits? ## Exception Paths - Suspicious or damaged items are flagged and sent to Mail Supervisor for inspection before distribution. - Undeliverable mail (invalid recipient) is logged and returned to sender or held for pickup. - Outgoing parcels exceeding service limits are reweighed and reclassified or returned to sender department. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Incoming Mail: The Mail Clerk receives all physical mail items from postal carriers and courier services at the loading dock or mailroom. Each item is logged with a receipt timestamp. Parcels and letters are separated for sorting. 2. Sort and Log Mail: Using the recipient directory, the Mail Clerk sorts each item by department or individual. Barcodes are scanned and tracking information is entered into the Mail Tracking System. Items are grouped for distribution. 3. Verify Recipient and Condition: The Mail Clerk visually inspects each item for signs of damage, tampering, or suspicious content. The recipient address is cross-checked against the directory. Items that pass verification proceed to distribution; exceptions are flagged. 4. Escalate Exception Items: Items flagged as damaged, suspicious, or with invalid addresses are sent to the Mail Supervisor. The Supervisor inspects the item, decides whether to return to sender, hold for pickup, or dispose. A decision is recorded in the system. 5. Distribute to Recipients: Verified mail items are physically delivered to department mailrooms or placed in individual mailboxes. Delivery is confirmed by scanning or manual sign-off. The system updates the delivery status. 6. Collect Outgoing Mail: The Mail Clerk makes rounds to designated collection points across the institution to collect outgoing mail. Items are brought to the mailroom for processing. A collection log is updated. 7. Prepare Outgoing Dispatch: Each outgoing item is weighed, measured, and labelled with the appropriate service class (e.g., standard, express, courier). The service level is selected based on active service agreements. Parcels are sorted by carrier. 8. Verify Service Compliance: The Mail Tracking System automatically checks each outgoing parcel's weight, dimensions, and selected service class against the terms in the service agreements. Non-compliant items are flagged for reclassification or return. 9. Dispatch Outgoing Mail: Compliant outgoing items are handed off to the appropriate carrier. The Mail Clerk records the dispatch timestamp, carrier details, and tracking numbers in the system. A dispatch report is generated for record-keeping.
