Commercial Opportunity Identification ()
## Purpose Commercial Opportunity Identification scans internal and external environments to detect potential commercial ventures, partnerships, or revenue streams. It leverages insights from academic research, industry trends, and stakeholder engagement, producing a pipeline of prospective opportunities for further evaluation. This process drives innovation and diversification of income streams, contributing to the institution's financial sustainability and market relevance. ## Roles - Innovation Manager: Leads the scanning and consolidation activities, facilitates stakeholder engagement - Research Liaison: Provides academic research outputs and technology transfer insights - Market Analyst: Conducts external market and industry trend analysis - System: Automated data aggregation and notification services ## Upstream Inputs - Academic Research Outputs: Patent filings, research publications, lab discoveries - Stakeholder Suggestions: Ideas from faculty, students, industry partners - Industry Trend Reports: Market analyses, competitor intelligence, technology forecasts ## Downstream Outputs - Opportunity Pipeline: Prioritised list of commercial opportunities for evaluation - Opportunity Briefs: One-page summaries for each identified opportunity ## Key Decisions - Does the opportunity align with institutional strategic priorities? - Is there sufficient market evidence to warrant further evaluation? - Does the opportunity have a clear commercialisation pathway? ## Exception Paths - No viable opportunities identified: Process concludes with a negative report and recommendation to revisit scanning parameters - Insufficient data to assess viability: Opportunity is placed in a 'watch list' for periodic re-evaluation - Stakeholder suggestion lacks commercial merit: Suggestion is logged with feedback provided to the originator ## Step Walkthrough 1. Gather Internal Insights: The Innovation Manager proactively reaches out to the Research Liaison for recent patent filings, research publications, and lab discoveries. Simultaneously, they solicit ideas from faculty, students, and industry partners through a structured suggestion intake process. All inputs are logged into a central opportunity register. 2. Scan External Environment: The Market Analyst conducts a systematic scan of published market analyses, competitor intelligence reports, and emerging technology forecasts. They identify key trends, gaps, and potential partnership opportunities. Findings are summarised in a market insight brief. 3. Consolidate Opportunity Inputs: The Innovation Manager combines the internal opportunity inputs with the external market signals into a single consolidated register. Duplicates are removed, and each entry is tagged with source and preliminary notes. The register forms the basis for viability assessment. 4. Assess Commercial Viability: The Innovation Manager reviews each entry in the consolidated register against three criteria: alignment with institutional strategic priorities, strength of market evidence, and clarity of commercialisation pathway. Opportunities that meet all criteria proceed; those that do not are either rejected or placed on a watch list. 5. Prioritise Opportunity Pipeline: The Innovation Manager scores each viable opportunity based on estimated revenue potential, strategic fit, time-to-market, and resource requirements. Opportunities are ranked and grouped into priority tiers (high, medium, low). The final pipeline is prepared for documentation. 6. Document and Distribute Pipeline: The Innovation Manager creates a one-page brief for each prioritised opportunity, summarising the opportunity, rationale, and recommended next steps. The full pipeline and briefs are distributed to the Commercial Sourcing team for downstream evaluation. A copy is archived for audit and reporting purposes. 7. Log Non-Viable Feedback: For each non-viable opportunity, the Innovation Manager logs the reason for rejection and, where applicable, provides constructive feedback to the stakeholder who suggested it. Opportunities with insufficient data are placed on a watch list for periodic re-evaluation.
