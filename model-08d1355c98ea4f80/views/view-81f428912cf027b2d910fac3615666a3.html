Curriculum Retirement Mgt ()
## Purpose This process ensures that curricula which are no longer viable—due to low enrolment, outdated content, or strategic realignment—are retired in a controlled, compliant manner. It balances academic portfolio optimisation with the duty to support currently enrolled students through to completion. ## Roles - Curriculum Manager: Initiates and coordinates the retirement process - Academic Dean: Reviews and approves the retirement plan - Registrar: Manages student progression data and communication - Quality Assurance Officer: Ensures regulatory compliance - System: Automates notifications and document archiving ## Upstream Inputs - Curriculum Performance Report - Retirement Request Form - Regulatory Compliance Checklist - Student Enrolment Data ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Retirement Plan - Retirement Communication Package - Archived Curriculum Record - Student Progression Tracking Report ## Key Decisions - Whether the curriculum meets retirement criteria (performance, relevance, regulatory fit) - Whether the retirement plan adequately addresses student progression pathways - Whether identified risks are acceptable or require mitigation ## Exception Paths - If the curriculum does not meet retirement criteria, the request is rejected and the initiator is notified with reasons - If the retirement plan has unacceptable risks, it is sent back for revision with specific remediation instructions - If student progression issues are identified, a separate student transition plan is triggered ## Step Walkthrough 1. Submit Retirement Request: The Curriculum Manager compiles a retirement request that includes performance metrics, enrolment trends, and a rationale for retirement. This request is submitted through the curriculum management system and triggers the review workflow. 2. Review Retirement Criteria: The Academic Dean examines the submitted request to determine if the curriculum meets the established retirement criteria, including low enrolment, outdated content, or strategic misalignment. A decision is made to either proceed or reject. 3. Develop Retirement Plan: Upon approval, the Curriculum Manager develops a comprehensive retirement plan that includes phased timelines, student progression pathways, regulatory compliance steps, and resource reallocation details. The plan is documented in the system. 4. Assess Plan Risks: The Quality Assurance Officer reviews the draft retirement plan for academic, regulatory, and operational risks. A risk assessment report is produced, highlighting any issues that need mitigation before final approval. 5. Approve Retirement Plan: The Academic Dean reviews the risk assessment report alongside the retirement plan. If risks are acceptable and the plan is sound, it is approved. If not, the plan is returned with specific revision instructions. 6. Communicate Retirement Timeline: The Registrar prepares and distributes a communication package that includes the retirement timeline, affected curricula, student transition arrangements, and contact points. Notifications are sent to academic departments, administrative units, and relevant committees. 7. Track Student Progression: The Registrar sets up tracking for all students currently enrolled in the retiring curriculum. Progress is monitored against the retirement timeline to ensure every student has a clear path to completion or a suitable alternative. 8. Archive Curriculum Record: Once all student progression is resolved and the retirement timeline is complete, the System automatically archives the curriculum record, including all associated documentation, approvals, and communications. The active record is closed and moved to long-term storage.
