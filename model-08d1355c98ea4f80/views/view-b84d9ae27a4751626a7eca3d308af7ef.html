## Purpose Estate Development & Management plans, coordinates, and oversees construction and refurbishment projects across the university estate. It consumes strategic plans, funding allocations, and compliance standards, delivering completed infrastructure aligned with institutional goals. This process ensures that physical assets meet current and future academic, research, and community needs. ## Roles - **Estates Director**: Approves project initiation and major stage gates - **Project Manager**: Coordinates day-to-day project activities and reporting - **Compliance Officer**: Reviews regulatory and safety compliance - **Finance Officer**: Manages funding allocation and budget tracking - **System**: Automated notifications and document management ## Upstream Inputs - Strategic Plans - Funding Allocations - Compliance Standards - Space Utilisation Reports ## Downstream Outputs - Completed Infrastructure - Project Handover Documentation - As-Built Drawings - Post-Project Review Report ## Key Decisions - Whether to proceed with a project based on feasibility assessment - Approval of detailed design and budget - Acceptance of completed works at handover ## Exception Paths - Feasibility assessment identifies critical risks → project returned for rework or cancelled - Steps: 1. Receive Strategic Inputs: The Estates Director reviews the latest strategic plans from university leadership and confirmed funding allocations. This step establishes the project pipeline and prioritisation criteria for the planning period. Key inputs include space utilisation reports and compliance standards. 2. Assess Project Feasibility: The Project Manager conducts a detailed feasibility study for each prioritised project. This includes site surveys, cost modelling, regulatory checks, and stakeholder consultations. The output is a feasibility report with risk ratings and recommendations. 3. Review Feasibility Report: The Compliance Officer checks regulatory and safety compliance aspects, while the Finance Officer reviews cost estimates and funding alignment. Both provide sign-off or raise issues for remediation. 4. Develop Detailed Design: Working with architects and engineers, the Project Manager develops full design specifications, construction drawings, and a detailed budget. This design is prepared for formal approval. 5. Approve Design and Budget: The Estates Director reviews the detailed design package and final budget estimate. If acceptable, approval is granted to proceed to construction. If issues are found, the package is returned for revision. 6. Execute Construction Works: Contractors execute the approved design under the supervision of the Project Manager. Regular progress reports, site inspections, and budget tracking occur throughout this phase. Any deviations are managed through change control. 7. Inspect Completed Works: The Compliance Officer conducts a final inspection of the completed construction or refurbishment. This includes safety checks, quality assurance, and verification against design specifications. Defects are logged for remediation. 8. Handover and Close-Out: The Project Manager assembles all handover documentation including as-built drawings, warranties, and maintenance schedules. A post-project review is conducted to capture lessons learned. The project is formally closed in the system.
