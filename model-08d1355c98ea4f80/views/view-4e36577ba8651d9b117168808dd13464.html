## Purpose This process manages the lifecycle of art and museum collections from acquisition through cataloguing, preservation, and display. It ensures that all cultural assets are properly documented, conserved, and made accessible for academic research, teaching, and public engagement, thereby enhancing the institution's cultural value and reputation. ## Roles - Collection Manager: Oversees acquisition, cataloguing, and inventory updates. - Curator: Reviews provenance and approves acquisitions. - Conservator: Assesses condition and performs preservation actions. - Exhibition Coordinator: Plans and schedules exhibitions. - System: Automated cataloguing and inventory updates. ## Upstream Inputs - Collection Acquisition Data: Details of new items proposed for acquisition. - Provenance Records: Historical ownership and authenticity documentation. - Conservation Reports: Previous condition assessments and treatment history. ## Downstream Outputs - Collection Inventory: Updated master list of all collection items with metadata. - Exhibition Schedule: Planned dates and item lists for upcoming exhibitions. - Conservation Log: Record of preservation actions taken. ## Key Decisions - Acquisition Approval: Whether to accept a new item based on provenance and collection fit. - Conservation Priority: Which items require immediate vs. deferred preservation. - Exhibition Selection: Which items are suitable for display based on condition and theme. ## Exception Paths - Provenance Issues Found: Item is rejected or sent for further investigation. - Conservation Required: Item is routed to preservation before cataloguing is finalised. - Item Unfit for Display: Item is excluded from exhibition and returned to storage. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Acquisition Request: The Collection Manager collects all incoming acquisition proposals, including item descriptions, provenance documentation, and any preliminary condition notes. This step initiates the process by gathering the necessary inputs for review. 2. Review Provenance: The Curator carefully reviews all provenance documentation, checking for gaps, inconsistencies, or red flags. This step ensures the item's history is complete and legally sound before proceeding. 3. Approve or Reject Acquisition: Based on the provenance assessment, the Curator makes a final decision. If approved, the item moves to cataloguing. If rejected, the item is returned to the donor or seller with a formal notification. 4. Catalogue Item: The Collection Management System automatically creates a new catalogue entry, including item title, artist, date, medium, dimensions, and provenance summary. A unique accession number is assigned to the item. 5. Assess Conservation Needs: The Conservator physically examines the item for damage, deterioration, or environmental risks. A condition report is generated, and a priority level for conservation is assigned. 6. Perform Preservation Actions: The Conservator carries out approved conservation treatments, such as cleaning, stabilisation, or repair. All actions are documented in the conservation log, and the item's condition is updated. 7. Update Collection Inventory: The Collection Management System integrates the catalogue record and conservation log to update the master inventory. The item's location (e.g., storage, gallery, or conservation lab) is recorded. 8. Plan Exhibition Display: The Exhibition Coordinator reviews the collection inventory and selects items that fit upcoming exhibition themes. Condition and conservation status are checked to ensure items are display-ready. 9. Finalise Exhibition Schedule: The Exhibition Coordinator confirms dates, item lists, and gallery assignments. The schedule is published internally and externally, and items are moved to display locations.
