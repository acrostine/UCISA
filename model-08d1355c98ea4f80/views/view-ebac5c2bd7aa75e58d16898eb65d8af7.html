Knowledge Mgt ()
## Purpose This process ensures that valuable institutional knowledge — including best practices, research findings, and operational expertise — is systematically captured, validated, stored, and shared across the organisation. It supports continuous improvement and reduces knowledge loss due to staff turnover. ## Roles - **Contributor**: Academic staff, researcher, or administrator who submits a knowledge asset - **Knowledge Manager**: Reviews submissions for quality and relevance, classifies content, and manages the repository - **System**: Automates notifications, metadata tagging, and archival triggers ## Upstream Inputs - Knowledge submission from contributor (document, video, case study, etc.) - Metadata provided by contributor (title, category, keywords) ## Downstream Outputs - Published knowledge asset in the central repository - Notification to contributor (approval or rework request) - Archived or updated asset after periodic review ## Key Decisions - Does the submission meet quality and relevance criteria? - Which category and tags should be assigned? - Is the asset still current during periodic review? ## Exception Paths - Submission rejected due to poor quality or irrelevance → contributor reworks and resubmits - Published asset found outdated during review → asset is archived or updated ## Step Walkthrough 1. **Submit Knowledge Asset**: Contributor uploads a knowledge asset and provides metadata via the knowledge portal. 2. **Review Submission**: Knowledge Manager evaluates the asset for quality, accuracy, and alignment with institutional priorities. 3. **Classify and Tag Asset**: Knowledge Manager assigns category, keywords, and access level to the approved asset. 4. **Publish to Repository**: System publishes the classified asset to the central knowledge repository and notifies the contributor. 5. **Periodic Review Check**: System triggers a scheduled review of published assets to assess currency and relevance. 6. **Archive or Update Asset**: Knowledge Manager either archives outdated assets or requests updates from the original contributor. Steps: 1. Submit Knowledge Asset: The contributor accesses the knowledge management portal, selects the appropriate content type (e.g., document, video, case study), and uploads the file along with required metadata such as title, description, category, and keywords. The system validates the submission format and stores it temporarily pending review. 2. Review Submission: The Knowledge Manager opens the submitted asset in the review queue. They assess the content for accuracy, completeness, alignment with institutional goals, and absence of sensitive information. Based on this evaluation, they either approve or reject the submission. 3. Rework Submission: The contributor receives a notification with the Knowledge Manager's feedback and rejection reason. They revise the asset accordingly — correcting errors, adding missing information, or improving clarity — and resubmit it through the portal for another review cycle. 4. Classify and Tag Asset: For approved submissions, the Knowledge Manager selects the appropriate category, adds relevant keywords and tags, sets the access level (e.g., public, internal, restricted), and links related assets if applicable. This step ensures the asset is discoverable and properly contextualised. 5. Publish to Repository: The system moves the classified asset from the staging area to the live knowledge repository, making it searchable and accessible to authorised users. An automated notification is sent to the contributor confirming publication and providing a link to the asset. 6. Periodic Review Check: Based on a predefined schedule (e.g., quarterly or annually), the system generates a list of assets due for review. It sends a notification to the Knowledge Manager with the list and prompts a currency assessment. 7. Archive or Update Asset: The Knowledge Manager reviews each flagged asset. If the content is still current and accurate, they mark it as reviewed and reset the review timer. If outdated, they either archive the asset (removing it from active search but preserving it for audit) or contact the original contributor to submit an updated version.
