It ensures that policies are legally compliant, operationally relevant, and effectively communicated to all stakeholders, thereby supporting the institution's governance and risk management objectives. ## Roles - Policy Owner: Initiates and owns the policy content. - Legal Counsel: Reviews for legal and regulatory compliance. - Governance Committee: Approves final policy. - Policy Administrator: Manages the repository and communication. - System: Automated notifications and document management. ## Upstream Inputs - Legislative Update Notification - Stakeholder Policy Request - Institutional Goal Directive - Existing Policy Review Schedule ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Policy Document - Policy Communication Announcement - Policy Repository Update Record - Review Schedule Entry ## Key Decisions - Whether the policy need is urgent or can follow the standard timeline. - Whether the draft policy meets all legal and regulatory requirements. - Whether to approve, reject, or return the policy for revision. - Whether the policy requires immediate communication or phased rollout. ## Exception Paths - If legal review identifies critical compliance gaps, the draft is returned to the Policy Owner for rework. - If the Governance Committee rejects the policy, it is returned to the Policy Owner with documented reasons. - If a policy is deemed urgent, the review and approval timeline is compressed, bypassing non-critical steps. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Identify Policy Need: The process is initiated by a trigger such as a legislative update, a formal request from a stakeholder, or a directive from institutional leadership. The Policy Owner logs the request in the policy management system, capturing the source, urgency, and scope of the required policy. 2. Draft Policy Document: The Policy Owner develops the policy content, ensuring it aligns with institutional goals and addresses the identified need. The draft includes the policy's purpose, scope, definitions, procedures, and responsibilities. The draft is saved in the policy management system. 3. Review Draft by Legal Counsel: The draft policy is routed to Legal Counsel for a thorough review. Legal Counsel checks for alignment with current laws, regulations, and sector standards. They provide a written assessment, either approving the draft or detailing required changes. 4. Assess Review Outcome: The policy management system checks the outcome of the legal review. If the assessment is positive, the process moves to governance approval. If issues are found, the process branches to a rework step. 5. Rework Policy Draft: The Policy Owner receives the detailed feedback from Legal Counsel and revises the policy draft accordingly. All changes are documented. The revised draft is then resubmitted for another legal review. 6. Submit for Governance Approval: Once the legal review is passed, the Policy Owner formally submits the policy draft to the Governance Committee for final approval. The submission includes the draft, the legal review assessment, and a summary of changes. 7. Evaluate Approval Decision: The Governance Committee reviews the submission package. They evaluate the policy's alignment with institutional strategy, risk posture, and operational impact. The committee votes to approve, reject, or request modifications. 8. Address Governance Feedback: If the Governance Committee rejects the policy or requests changes, the Policy Owner reviews the feedback, makes the necessary revisions, and resubmits the draft for another committee review. 9. Publish and Communicate Policy: The Policy Administrator publishes the final approved policy to the central policy repository. A communication is sent to all relevant stakeholders via email and the intranet, summarizing the policy's key points and effective date. 10. Schedule Periodic Review: The Policy Administrator schedules a periodic review date in the policy management system, typically within 1-3 years. This ensures the policy remains current and compliant with evolving regulations and institutional needs.
