## Purpose This process governs the handling of formal disputes within the higher education institution, covering contractual, employment, and academic conflicts. It ensures that each case is assessed, triaged, and resolved through the most appropriate mechanism—negotiation, mediation, or litigation—while protecting institutional interests and fostering constructive outcomes. ## Roles - Dispute Intake Clerk: Receives and logs incoming dispute submissions - Legal Officer: Reviews case details, determines resolution pathway, and facilitates resolution - Mediator (External or Internal): Conducts mediation sessions when required - Legal Counsel: Represents the institution in formal legal proceedings - Records Manager: Archives final case documentation ## Upstream Inputs - Formal Dispute Submission (case details, parties involved, nature of conflict) - Relevant Policies and Contracts (employment agreements, academic regulations, vendor contracts) - Stakeholder Inputs (statements from involved parties, witness accounts) - Prior Correspondence (emails, letters, meeting notes) ## Downstream Outputs - Resolution Agreement or Settlement Document - Legal Proceedings Record (if escalated) - Corrective Action Plan (if applicable) - Case Closure Report - Archived Case File ## Key Decisions - Determine whether the dispute is eligible for mediation or requires immediate legal escalation - Assess whether the proposed resolution is fair, legally sound, and aligned with institutional policy - Decide if corrective actions are needed to prevent recurrence ## Exception Paths - If mediation fails, the case is escalated to formal legal proceedings - If the dispute involves imminent legal risk, it bypasses mediation and proceeds directly to litigation - If the parties cannot agree on a mediator, an external neutral mediator is appointed ## Step Walkthrough 1. Triage Resolution Pathway: The Legal Officer reviews the logged dispute to assess its complexity, urgency, and legal risk. Based on this assessment, they decide whether the case should proceed to negotiation, mediation, or direct legal proceedings. Cases involving imminent legal risk are flagged for immediate escalation. 3. Review Case Details and Policies: The Legal Officer conducts a thorough review of all applicable policies, contracts, and stakeholder statements. This step ensures the institution's position is well-understood and that any resolution aligns with legal obligations and institutional values. 4. Facilitate Mediation Session: The Mediator (internal or external) facilitates a confidential mediation session. Both parties present their positions, and the mediator guides discussion toward a mutually acceptable resolution. The session is documented, and any tentative agreement is recorded in principle. 5. Assess Mediation Outcome: The Legal Officer reviews the mediation outcome to determine if a settlement was achieved. They assess whether the proposed terms are fair, legally sound, and aligned with institutional policy. If the outcome is acceptable, the case proceeds to documentation; if not, it escalates to legal proceedings. 6. Escalate to Legal Proceedings: If mediation fails or the case was triaged directly to litigation, Legal Counsel prepares and files the necessary legal documents. The institution is represented in court, tribunal, or arbitration. All proceedings are recorded and monitored for compliance with legal deadlines. 7. Document Resolution Outcome: The Legal Officer drafts and finalizes the resolution document—whether a settlement agreement, consent order, or court judgment. All parties sign where required, and the document is stored in the case file. This step ensures a clear, enforceable record of the outcome. 8. Implement Corrective Actions: If the dispute revealed policy gaps, procedural weaknesses, or recurring issues, the Legal Officer develops a corrective action plan. This may include policy updates, training, or process changes. The plan is submitted for approval and tracked to completion. 9. Archive Case File: The Records Manager compiles the complete case file—including intake records, mediation notes, legal documents, and the final resolution—and archives it in the institutional records system. The file is retained per the institution's records retention policy.
