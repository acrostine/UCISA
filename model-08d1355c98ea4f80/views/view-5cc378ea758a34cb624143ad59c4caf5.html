## Purpose Timetabling Management ensures that teaching sessions are scheduled efficiently by balancing staff availability, room capacities, and course requirements. The process minimizes scheduling conflicts and maximizes resource utilization, ultimately enhancing the student learning experience through reliable and accessible schedules. ## Roles - Timetable Coordinator: Oversees the scheduling process and resolves conflicts - Academic Administrator: Collects scheduling requirements from departments - System: Automated scheduling engine and conflict detection tool - Department Head: Approves final timetable for their department ## Upstream Inputs - Course Offering Data: List of courses, sections, and session types - Staff Availability Data: Lecturer schedules and preferences - Facility Capacity Data: Room sizes, equipment, and virtual platform limits - Academic Calendar: Term dates, holidays, and exam periods ## Downstream Outputs - Published Timetable: Final schedule distributed to students and staff - Room Allocation Report: Utilization statistics for facilities - Conflict Resolution Log: Record of resolved and escalated conflicts ## Key Decisions - Whether to approve or reject a proposed timetable based on conflict severity - Whether to escalate unresolvable conflicts to department heads - Whether to accept or reject room reallocation proposals ## Exception Paths - Scheduling conflicts detected: triggers conflict resolution loop - Staff unavailability identified: triggers rework of session allocation - Room capacity exceeded: triggers room reallocation or session split - Department head rejects timetable: triggers full rework from scheduling ## Step Walkthrough 1. Collect Requirements: The Timetable Coordinator collects all necessary scheduling inputs including course offerings from academic departments, staff availability and preferences from HR systems, and facility capacity data from estates management. This data forms the foundation for timetable generation. 2. Generate Draft Timetable: The timetabling system processes the scheduling requirements package and generates a draft timetable. The algorithm assigns sessions to time slots and rooms while respecting staff availability and room capacities. The draft timetable is produced for review. 3. Check for Conflicts: The system runs a conflict detection algorithm against the draft timetable. It checks for double-booked rooms, overlapping staff assignments, and time slot clashes. The output is a conflict report highlighting any issues found. 4. Resolve Conflicts: The Timetable Coordinator reviews the conflict report and manually adjusts session assignments to resolve each conflict. This may involve swapping rooms, changing time slots, or reallocating staff. The adjusted timetable is prepared for review. 5. Review Timetable: The Academic Administrator examines the adjusted timetable to ensure all sessions are correctly scheduled, no new conflicts have been introduced, and all requirements are met. The review confirms the timetable is ready for departmental approval. 6. Approve Timetable: The Department Head reviews the final timetable and either approves it for publication or rejects it with feedback. Approval confirms the timetable meets departmental needs. Rejection triggers a rework loop back to the scheduling stage. 7. Publish Timetable: The timetabling system makes the approved timetable available to all stakeholders. Students can view their personal schedules, staff can see their teaching assignments, and room bookings are confirmed. The publication completes the process.
