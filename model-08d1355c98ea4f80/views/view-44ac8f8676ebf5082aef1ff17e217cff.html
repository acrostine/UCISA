Procurement & Purchasing Mgt ()
## Purpose This process governs the end-to-end acquisition of goods and services, from receipt of a requisition through vendor evaluation, purchase order creation, approval, and issuance. It ensures compliance with procurement policies, optimises value for money, and produces the documentation needed to formalise commitments with vendors. ## Roles - Procurement Officer: Reviews requisitions, evaluates vendor assessments, creates purchase orders, and manages contract documentation. - Approver (Manager/Director): Reviews and approves purchase orders above threshold. - Requisitioner: Submits the initial requisition and reworks it if necessary. - System (ERP/Procurement System): Automates vendor evaluation scoring, purchase order generation, and document storage. ## Upstream Inputs - Requisition (from Requisitioner) - Vendor Evaluation (from Vendor Management process) ## Downstream Outputs - Approved Purchase Order (to Vendor) - Contract Documentation (to Vendor and Legal/Records) - Rejected Requisition Notification (to Requisitioner) ## Key Decisions - Does the requisition comply with policy and contain all required information? - Does the vendor evaluation meet the minimum scoring threshold? - Does the purchase order require additional approval based on value or risk? ## Exception Paths - If the requisition is incomplete or non-compliant, it is returned to the Requisitioner for rework. - If the vendor evaluation fails to meet the threshold, the requisition is rejected and sent back for rework with feedback. - If the purchase order exceeds the approval threshold, it is routed to a higher-level Approver. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Requisition: The Procurement Officer accesses the procurement system to retrieve the submitted requisition and the associated vendor evaluation from the Vendor Management process. These inputs form the basis for all subsequent procurement activities. 2. Review Requisition Compliance: The Procurement Officer examines the requisition to ensure it adheres to institutional procurement policies, contains accurate item descriptions, correct budget codes, and appropriate justifications. If any issues are found, the requisition is flagged for rework. 3. Rework Requisition: The Requisitioner receives feedback on the compliance issues and updates the requisition accordingly. Once corrected, the requisition is resubmitted for re-review by the Procurement Officer. 4. Assess Vendor Evaluation: The Procurement Officer reviews the vendor evaluation report, checking scores for price, quality, delivery, and compliance. If the vendor meets the minimum threshold, the process continues; otherwise, the requisition is returned for rework with vendor feedback. 5. Address Vendor Issues: The Requisitioner receives details on why the vendor evaluation failed and works with the vendor or selects an alternative vendor. The updated requisition and new vendor evaluation are resubmitted for reassessment. 6. Create Purchase Order: Using the compliant requisition and the passed vendor evaluation, the procurement system automatically generates a purchase order. The officer reviews the draft for accuracy before submission for approval. 7. Approve Purchase Order: The purchase order is routed to the Approver. If the value exceeds a predefined threshold or involves high-risk items, it is escalated to a senior manager. The Approver may approve or reject the order. 8. Issue Purchase Order: The Procurement Officer issues the approved purchase order to the vendor via the procurement system. The system records the issuance date and updates the order status. 9. Generate Contract Documentation: The procurement system generates formal contract documentation based on the purchase order and vendor terms. The documents are stored in the contract repository for audit and legal reference.
