Infrastructure Lifecycle Mgt ()
## Purpose Infrastructure Lifecycle Management oversees the planning, deployment, maintenance, and decommissioning of ICT hardware and network components. It consumes capacity plans, technology standards, and operational data, delivering resilient and scalable infrastructure. This process ensures that physical and virtual ICT assets effectively support institutional demands and evolving technological landscapes. ## Roles - Infrastructure Architect - Capacity Planner - Procurement Officer - Deployment Engineer - Maintenance Technician - Change Manager - Security Officer - System (automated monitoring tools) ## Upstream Inputs - Capacity Plan - Technology Standards - Operational Data (incident, performance, utilisation) - Asset Register - Budget Allocation ## Downstream Outputs - Deployed Infrastructure (servers, network, storage) - Updated Asset Register - Maintenance Schedule - Decommissioning Certificate - Capacity Forecast Update ## Key Decisions - Whether to approve a new infrastructure deployment or upgrade - Whether to approve a decommissioning request - Whether an asset requires emergency replacement or can follow standard lifecycle - Whether maintenance findings require escalation to change management ## Exception Paths - If capacity review identifies a critical gap, an emergency procurement and deployment bypasses standard approval - If deployment fails validation, the asset is quarantined and a remediation step is triggered - If maintenance health check reveals a critical vulnerability, an immediate patch or replacement is initiated - If decommissioning request is rejected, the asset remains in service with a revised maintenance plan ## Step Walkthrough 1. Update Capacity Forecast: Capacity Planner incorporates decommissioning and new deployments into the forward capacity plan; outputs updated capacity forecast. Steps: 1. Review Capacity and Demand: The Capacity Planner reviews the current capacity plan, technology standards, and operational data including utilisation trends and incident reports. This analysis identifies where infrastructure is over- or under-provisioned and where new deployments or upgrades are needed. A capacity gap report is produced to inform planning decisions. 2. Develop Deployment Plan: Based on the capacity gap report, the Infrastructure Architect develops a deployment plan specifying hardware/network specifications, implementation timeline, resource requirements, and budget estimates. The plan aligns with technology standards and security policies. It is documented for review and approval. 3. Review and Approve Plan: The Infrastructure Architect and Change Manager jointly review the deployment plan against technology standards, security policies, and change management criteria. They evaluate cost, timeline, and operational impact. The decision determines whether the plan proceeds to procurement or requires revision. 4. Procure Infrastructure: The Procurement Officer issues purchase orders for the approved hardware, software, and network components. They manage vendor communication, track delivery schedules, and ensure all items meet specified standards. A delivery schedule is produced and shared with the deployment team. 5. Deploy and Validate Infrastructure: The Deployment Engineer physically or virtually installs the infrastructure, configures network settings, and integrates with existing systems. A validation test is run to confirm performance, security, and connectivity. If validation passes, the asset is commissioned; if it fails, remediation is required. 6. Remediate Deployment Issues: The Deployment Engineer diagnoses and fixes issues identified during validation, such as misconfiguration, driver incompatibility, or network conflicts. After remediation, the infrastructure is re-tested. The step loops back to the validation step until all issues are resolved. 7. Perform Proactive Maintenance: The Maintenance Technician performs routine health checks, firmware updates, patch management, and performance tuning on active infrastructure assets. Findings are logged in a maintenance report. Critical issues are escalated to change management for emergency action. 8. Assess Decommissioning Request: When an asset reaches end-of-life or is no longer needed, a decommissioning request is raised. The Infrastructure Architect and Security Officer assess the request for data security, service impact, and compliance. If approved, decommissioning proceeds; if rejected, the asset is retained with a revised maintenance plan. 9. Execute Decommissioning: The Maintenance Technician securely erases all data from the asset, removes it from the network, and physically or virtually decommissions it. The asset register is updated, and a decommissioning certificate is issued to confirm completion. 10. Update Capacity Forecast: The Capacity Planner updates the capacity forecast to reflect the newly deployed infrastructure and decommissioned assets. This ensures future capacity planning is accurate and aligned with institutional demand. The updated forecast is published for stakeholders.
