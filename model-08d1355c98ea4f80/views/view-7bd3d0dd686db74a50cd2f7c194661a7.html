Records Mgt ()
## Purpose This process ensures that all institutional records, whether physical or digital, are systematically classified, retained for the required period, and either disposed of or archived in compliance with legal and regulatory frameworks. Proper records management reduces legal risk, supports internal and external audits, and preserves the institution's historical knowledge. ## Roles - Records Officer: Classifies records, manages retention schedules, and initiates disposal or archival actions. - Records Manager: Reviews and approves disposal or archival recommendations, ensures policy compliance. - System: EDRMS (Electronic Document and Records Management System) automates retention calculations and logs all actions. ## Upstream Inputs - Physical Records: Paper documents, files, and folders received from departments. - Digital Records: Electronic files, emails, and database exports received from departments. - Retention Schedule: Legal and regulatory guidelines defining retention periods for each record type. ## Downstream Outputs - Classified Records: Records tagged with retention category and expiry date in the EDRMS. - Archived Records: Records moved to long-term storage (physical or digital archive). - Disposal Certificate: Formal documentation confirming records have been destroyed in compliance with policy. - Audit Log: Complete history of all classification, retention, and disposal actions. ## Key Decisions - Classification Assignment: Which retention category applies to the incoming record? - Disposal vs. Archival: At the end of the retention period, should the record be destroyed or permanently preserved? - Approval of Disposal: Does the Records Manager authorise the destruction of the record? ## Exception Paths - Unclassified Record: If a record does not match any category in the retention schedule, it is flagged for review and a new classification may be created. - Disposal Rejected: If the Records Manager rejects a disposal request, the record is retained and re-evaluated at a later date. - Record Not Found: If a record scheduled for disposal cannot be located, an investigation is initiated. ## Step Walkthrough 1. Receive Record: The Records Officer receives a record from a department, either as a physical document or a digital file. The record is logged into the Electronic Document and Records Management System (EDRMS) with basic metadata such as source department, date received, and record type. 2. Classify Record: The Records Officer reviews the record's content and matches it to the appropriate category in the institutional retention schedule. The classification determines how long the record must be kept and whether it is eligible for disposal or permanent archival. 3. Calculate Retention End Date: Based on the classification assigned and the record's creation date, the EDRMS automatically calculates the retention end date. This date is stored as metadata on the record and triggers future review alerts. 4. Monitor Retention Period: The EDRMS continuously monitors all classified records. When a record's retention end date is reached, the system generates an alert for the Records Officer to review the record for disposal or archival. 5. Review for Disposal: Upon receiving the retention expiry alert, the Records Officer examines the record and determines whether it should be destroyed (disposal) or preserved permanently (archival). This decision is based on the retention schedule and any legal holds. 6. Approve Disposal: The Records Manager reviews the disposal recommendation from the Records Officer. If the record is eligible and no legal holds apply, the manager approves the disposal. If there are concerns, the manager rejects the request and the record is retained for further review. 7. Dispose Record: With approval granted, the Records Officer physically shreds the document or permanently deletes the digital file. The EDRMS logs the destruction and generates a Disposal Certificate as formal evidence of compliance. 8. Archive Record: If the Records Officer recommends archival instead of disposal, the record is moved to a long-term storage location (physical archive or digital archive). The EDRMS status is updated to 'Archived' and the record is preserved indefinitely.
