Occupational Health ()
## Purpose This process ensures that employee health risks are systematically identified, assessed, and mitigated through structured interventions. It leverages medical reports, incident logs, and regulatory standards to produce health assessments, risk analyses, and safety interventions, ultimately reducing absenteeism and supporting statutory compliance. ## Roles - Occupational Health Nurse - Health & Safety Manager - System (for automated notifications and record-keeping) - Employee (as information source) ## Upstream Inputs - Employee Health Records - Incident Logs - Environmental Data - Regulatory Standards ## Downstream Outputs - Health Assessments - Risk Analyses - Workplace Safety Interventions - Compliance Reports ## Key Decisions - Whether a reported incident or health record requires a full risk assessment - Whether identified risks meet the threshold for intervention - Whether the intervention plan is approved for execution ## Exception Paths - If health records are incomplete, the process loops back to request additional information from the employee - If risk assessment identifies critical hazards, immediate escalation to the Health & Safety Manager occurs - If intervention plan is rejected, it is reworked and resubmitted ## Step Walkthrough 1. Document and Report Outcomes: All actions and results are recorded, and a compliance report is generated for regulatory and management review. Steps: 1. Receive Health Record: The process starts when an employee health record or an incident log is submitted via the HR system. The data is validated for completeness and stored for assessment. This step ensures all relevant information is available for subsequent risk evaluation. 2. Assess Health Risk: The Occupational Health Nurse reviews the validated record, comparing it with established risk criteria. This step identifies potential health hazards and determines the severity of the risk. The outcome is a preliminary risk classification that guides further action. 3. Review Regulatory Standards: The Health & Safety Manager retrieves relevant regulatory standards from the compliance database. This step ensures that the risk assessment aligns with statutory requirements and industry best practices. The output is a set of compliance criteria used in the next decision. 4. Determine Intervention Need: Based on the preliminary risk classification and compliance criteria, the Occupational Health Nurse determines whether the risk level warrants a formal intervention. This is a critical decision point that separates routine monitoring from active mitigation. 5. Plan Safety Intervention: If intervention is needed, the Occupational Health Nurse creates a plan specifying actions, responsible parties, resources, and timelines. The plan is designed to mitigate the identified risks effectively. This step produces a draft intervention plan for approval. 6. Approve Intervention Plan: The Health & Safety Manager reviews the draft plan for feasibility, cost, and compliance. If satisfactory, the plan is approved for execution. If not, it is returned with feedback for revision. 7. Execute Intervention: The approved plan is put into action. This may involve training sessions, equipment upgrades, or changes to workplace procedures. The Occupational Health Nurse coordinates with relevant departments to ensure timely execution. 8. Document and Report: All actions taken, results observed, and any residual risks are documented. A compliance report is generated for regulatory bodies and management review. This step ensures traceability and supports continuous improvement.
