Assessment Administration ()
## Purpose Assessment Administration organises and manages assessment schedules, submission guidelines, and compliance with academic regulations. It consumes course requirements, institutional policies, and student enrolment data to ensure assessments align with curriculum standards. This process produces assessment timetables, registration records, and administrative documentation. It enables efficient coordination and reduces risks of procedural errors, supporting academic integrity and student experience. ## Roles - Assessment Coordinator: Oversees scheduling, compliance review, and final approval - Academic Registrar: Provides institutional policies and validates regulatory compliance - System: Automates timetable generation and notification distribution - Teaching Staff: Submits course requirements and receives final schedules ## Upstream Inputs - Course Requirements: Module outlines, assessment types, weighting, and deadlines - Institutional Policies: Academic regulations, submission guidelines, and integrity rules - Student Enrolment Data: Enrolled student lists, cohorts, and special accommodations ## Downstream Outputs - Assessment Timetable: Published schedule of all assessments with dates, rooms, and invigilators - Registration Records: Confirmed student registrations per assessment - Administrative Documentation: Compliance checklists, resource allocation logs, and approval records ## Key Decisions - Whether the proposed schedule complies with all academic regulations - Whether resources (rooms, invigilators, equipment) are sufficient for each assessment - Whether to approve the final timetable for publication ## Exception Paths - Schedule conflicts with institutional policies: flagged for rework and re-submission - Resource shortages identified: escalated to Academic Registrar for resolution - Validation failures: returned to Assessment Coordinator for correction ## Step Walkthrough 1. Gather Course Requirements: The Assessment Coordinator requests and collects course requirements from each teaching staff member. This includes module outlines, assessment types (exam, coursework, practical), weightings, and preferred deadlines. All data is compiled into a central working document for scheduling. 2. Review Institutional Policies: The Assessment Coordinator reviews current institutional policies including academic regulations, submission guidelines, late penalties, and integrity rules. Key constraints such as maximum assessments per day, minimum spacing between exams, and special accommodation rules are noted for schedule design. 3. Load Enrolment Data: The System automatically imports student enrolment data including enrolled cohorts, module registrations, and any special accommodation requirements (e.g., extra time, separate rooms). This data is used to size rooms and allocate invigilators appropriately. 4. Define Assessment Schedule: The Assessment Coordinator creates a draft assessment timetable using the compiled course requirements, policy constraints, and enrolment data. Dates, time slots, room assignments, and invigilator allocations are proposed. The draft is prepared for compliance validation. 5. Validate Schedule Compliance: The Assessment Coordinator systematically checks the draft timetable against all documented policy constraints. Conflicts such as overlapping assessments for the same cohort, insufficient spacing, or violations of maximum assessment rules are identified and flagged for correction. 6. Allocate Resources: The System automatically assigns rooms and invigilators to each assessment slot based on room capacity, student numbers, and invigilator availability. Special accommodation rooms are prioritised. A resource allocation report is generated for review. 7. Review Resource Allocation: The Assessment Coordinator reviews the resource allocation report to ensure all assessments have adequate rooms and invigilators. Any shortages or conflicts (e.g., double-booked rooms) are identified. If issues exist, the coordinator adjusts allocations or escalates to the Academic Registrar. 8. Approve Timetable: The Academic Registrar reviews the final draft timetable, compliance validation results, and resource allocation. If all criteria are met, the timetable is formally approved. If not, it is returned with comments for revision. 9. Publish Timetable: The System publishes the approved timetable to the institutional portal and sends notifications to all teaching staff and enrolled students. The timetable is made available for download and online viewing. Registration records are automatically generated. 10. Archive Documentation: The System archives all administrative documentation including the final timetable, compliance validation results, resource allocation logs, and approval records. These are stored in a secure repository with audit trail capabilities for future reference and regulatory review.
